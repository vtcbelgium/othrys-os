# HYPERION BUYER & ADOPTION STUDY 001

> Status: COMMERCIAL RESEARCH — not permission to monetize.
> Date: 2026-09-08
> Scope: consumer age cohorts, older adults, workers, small businesses, enterprises, upgrade triggers, fast-paying segments, low-touch product implications.

## Executive conclusion

The best commercial target is not simply “who uses AI most.” It is **who experiences a repeated problem, already trusts software enough to use it, sees a measurable gain, and can upgrade without needing hand-holding**.

The strongest low-touch commercial zones are:

1. Professionals and power users aged roughly 25–49 using AI for work and repeated productivity tasks.
2. Students as rapid adopters and excellent product-validation users, but weaker direct willingness/ability to pay unless value is very concrete or institution-funded.
3. Small businesses, especially younger-owner and professional-services firms, when AI attaches directly to revenue, marketing, customer handling, software, reporting, cost reduction or repetitive admin.
4. Enterprises when a tool moves from personal productivity into workflow, governance, reliability, data access, procurement or measurable ROI. Enterprise spends far more, but sales cycles/support burden are higher.
5. Older adults are underpenetrated rather than irrelevant. The commercial opportunity is not “AI for seniors” as a chatbot pitch; it is simple, trustworthy, outcome-specific assistance with low setup and obvious utility.

Hyperion should optimize for **low-touch upgrade moments**, not maximum raw adoption.

## 1. Adoption by age

Current evidence shows a steep age gradient in chatbot use, but usage is rising everywhere.

Pew 2026 U.S. chatbot use:
- 18–29: 66%
- 30–49: 61%
- 50–64: 42%
- 65+: 23%

ChatGPT specifically:
- 18–29: 61%
- 30–49: 55%
- 50–64: 37%
- 65+: 19%

The most commercially attractive consumer band is not necessarily the youngest. Ages 30–49 combine high adoption with high work usage, purchasing power and repeated productivity needs.

Among employed adults, Pew reports chatbot work use at:
- 18–29: 41%
- 30–49: 43%
- 50–64: 29%
- 65+: 18%

### Hyperion interpretation

**18–29:** easiest to acquire, hardest to monetize broadly. Highly familiar with free AI; price-sensitive; strong for study, creation, job seeking, entertainment and experimentation. Best paid wedge: specific outcome, deadline, grade/career value, or bundled institution access.

**30–49:** strongest direct-to-consumer/prosumer commercial cohort. High work usage, disposable income, overloaded schedules, willingness to pay for time savings and professional advantage. Strongest targets: productivity, research, project memory, admin, career, automation, creator/business tools, cost-saving utilities.

**50–64:** smaller adoption but potentially good payer when problem is obvious and product is simple. Strong targets: work assistance, admin, finance organization, travel, document handling, personal knowledge, family organization, tech support.

**65+:** adoption still low and confidence is a major barrier. Pew reports only 6% are extremely/very confident with AI chatbots and 77% do not use them. The opportunity therefore sits in hiding the AI and selling the outcome. Avoid prompt-engineering interfaces, jargon, setup complexity and open-ended blank chat boxes.

## 2. Belgium signal

Deloitte Belgium's 2025 consumer study reports 56% of Belgian consumers have used generative AI, with 85% of students and 64% of working individuals having used it.

This means OTHRYS does not need to wait for AI awareness in Belgium. The commercial question is increasingly **conversion from free/general use to specific recurring value**.

Older Belgian users should not be written off as anti-tech. Deloitte reports strong adoption of other digital devices and platforms among Boomers, suggesting simplicity and perceived relevance matter more than age alone.

- faster completion;

A prettier chat window is not enough.

## 4. The consumer free-to-paid gap

Menlo Ventures estimates roughly 1.8B consumers use AI globally, but only around 3% pay for AI tools. It estimates about 81% of consumer AI spend goes to general assistants and only 19% to specialized AI products. Menlo also estimates ChatGPT converts roughly 5% of weekly active users to paid.

This is the central consumer monetization fact:

> **USAGE IS EASY. PAYMENT IS RARE.**

Hyperion should never use user count alone as evidence of commercial value.

### Consumer upgrade triggers

A free user becomes a plausible payer when one or more thresholds are crossed:

1. **Frequency threshold** — they use the product several times per week and losing it becomes annoying.
2. **Time-value threshold** — it reliably saves enough time that subscription cost feels trivial.
3. **Output-value threshold** — output affects grades, employment, money, clients, travel, purchases or other consequential decisions.
4. **Limit threshold** — free caps interrupt a workflow already proven valuable.
5. **Memory threshold** — the product has accumulated useful personal/project context the generic free tool lacks.
6. **Automation threshold** — the user no longer wants to manually repeat the task.
7. **Trust threshold** — source grounding, verification, privacy or reproducibility becomes important.
8. **Workflow threshold** — files, history, integrations, exports or recurring jobs become necessary.
9. **Professional threshold** — use shifts from curiosity to work/business where reliability matters.
10. **Switching-cost threshold** — the product becomes the user's organized operating layer.

The ideal OTHRYS free tier should let value become habitual, then charge at a **natural pain boundary**, not artificially cripple the first experience.

## 5. Who pays fastest among consumers

### Tier A — fastest plausible direct payers

**Working professionals / prosumers (roughly 25–49)**
Pay for:
- time savings;
- research;
- writing/output quality;
- coding and automation;
- project memory;
- career/job support;
- meeting/document handling;
- content production;
- specialized professional workflows.

Why fast: they can translate minutes saved into economic or career value.

**Creators / freelancers / solo operators**
Pay for:
- repeatable content/workflow generation;
- lead/research automation;
- client deliverables;
- image/video assistance;
- proposal/report generation;
- admin reduction;
- reusable workflow packs.

Why fast: tool cost can be compared directly with revenue or hours.

**Serious hobbyists / collectors / power users**
Pay when the product has unique data, organization, tracking, valuation, history or automation that generic AI cannot recreate easily.

### Tier B — strong users, weaker immediate payer

**Students**
Very high adoption. Payment friction is higher because income is lower and free alternatives are plentiful.
Best monetization routes later:
- low-cost monthly/semester plan;
- exam-season pass;
- institution/school licensing;
- parent-funded plan;
- premium memory, transcription, source-grounded study, quiz/gap analytics.

Study Buddy is strategically valuable even before monetization because it generates dogfood, retention and learning-effect evidence.

### Tier C — underserved but conversion requires product design

**50–64 and 65+**
Do not sell “AI.” Sell:
- organize my documents;
- explain this letter;
- prepare my trip;
- help compare purchases;
- find information in my files;
- keep family/admin records organized;
- guide me through recurring digital tasks;
- preserve memories/photos/history;
- help with hobbies/collections.

Upgrade triggers should be storage/history, family sharing, document volume, recurring assistance, backup/export and trusted sources—not model names or token limits.

## 6. Small-business adoption

U.S. Chamber research reports small-business AI use rose from 23% in 2023 to 40% in 2024 and 58% in 2025.

Small-business priorities remain pragmatic. In Q3 2025:
- 58% said marketing/sales is very important to invest in;
- 43% technology/software;
- 28% AI tools.

When asked where most investment is currently focused:
- marketing/sales: 36%;
- technology/software: 15%;
- AI tools: 13%.

Among firms focusing on technology/software, 60% prioritize business software, 36% AI, 32% data/analytics and 27% cybersecurity.

Owner age matters: younger owners place substantially greater importance on digital experience, software and AI than Baby Boomer+ owners.

### Hyperion interpretation

Businesses do not primarily buy “AI.” They buy:
- more leads/sales;
- faster response;
- lower labor/admin burden;
- fewer mistakes;
- better reporting;
- faster content creation;
- inventory/data insight;
- customer support;
- document processing;
- reliable software workflows.

Therefore OTHRYS should package business products around **jobs and economics**, not AI sophistication.

## 7. Which businesses pay fastest

### Fastest low-touch business buyers

1. **Professional services** — consultants, agencies, accountants, legal-adjacent, recruiters, technical services.
   - High value of time.
   - Document-heavy workflows.
   - Repeatable client outputs.
   - Already spend on software.
   - Strong fit for self-serve research/report/document/automation products.

2. **Software/development teams**
   - Proven high AI adoption.
   - Direct productivity metrics.
   - Will pay for coding, testing, reliability, observability, cost routing and evidence.
   - Strong OTHRYS fit: Tool Olympics, Evidence Gate, Repo Health, Project Memory, routing/cost intelligence.

3. **Sales/marketing/creator businesses**
   - Revenue connection is visible.
   - Pay for content, prospect research, campaign automation, enrichment, reporting and workflow packs.
   - Caveat: crowded market; differentiation must be measurable or niche-specific.

4. **Support-heavy businesses**
   - Pay when automation deflects repetitive tickets or accelerates agents.
   - Strong ROI but integrations/support can increase interaction burden.

5. **Data/document-heavy verticals**
   - Healthcare, finance, legal, insurance and regulated operations have high willingness to pay for specialized outcomes, but compliance and sales complexity are much higher. Good later Hyperion territory, not first low-touch target.

### Slower / harder targets

- very small traditional businesses with low digital maturity;
- businesses requiring extensive custom integration;
- buyers unable to measure saved time or revenue;
- sectors where every deployment becomes a consulting project;
- highly regulated workflows before OTHRYS has mature security/compliance evidence.

## 8. Business free-to-paid / small-to-large upgrade point

A business upgrades when AI stops being an individual experiment and becomes **operational dependency**.

Typical transitions:

### Stage 0 — curiosity
One employee uses free ChatGPT.

No strong willingness to pay beyond personal subscriptions.

### Stage 1 — repeated personal productivity
Several staff repeatedly use AI for writing, research, coding, meetings or documents.

Upgrade need:
- higher limits;
- better models;
- saved context;
- file handling;
- privacy;
- consistency.

### Stage 2 — workflow dependency
AI becomes embedded in a recurring job.

Upgrade need:
- automation;
- integrations;
- scheduling;
- batch operations;
- shared templates;
- monitoring;
- API access;
- role access.

### Stage 3 — team dependency
Multiple people depend on it.

Upgrade need:
- shared knowledge;
- permissions;
- central billing;
- audit trail;
- version control;
- team analytics;
- support/recovery.

### Stage 4 — business-critical dependency
Failure costs money or creates risk.

Upgrade need:
- evidence;
- SLA/reliability;
- fallback routing;
- cost controls;
- governance;
- incident handling;
- data policy;
- security;
- qualification;
- procurement evidence.

### Stage 5 — portfolio dependency
Many models/agents/tools exist across the company.

Upgrade need:
- control plane;
- inventory;
- vendor comparison;
- cost-per-outcome;
- regression monitoring;
- capability registry;
- policy enforcement;
- agent passports/A-BOM;
- enterprise-wide intelligence.

This progression maps almost perfectly onto OTHRYS architecture.

## 9. Enterprise spend: where serious money already goes

Menlo Ventures estimates enterprise generative-AI spend reached about $37B in 2025, with roughly $19B going to application-layer AI. Horizontal, departmental and vertical AI applications all grew strongly.

Practical enterprise use cases with demonstrated adoption include:
- code copilots;
- support chatbots;
- enterprise search/retrieval;
- data extraction/transformation;
- meeting summarization;
- healthcare documentation and workflow automation.

The common denominator is not novelty. It is **repeated work with measurable labor/productivity value**.

Deloitte's 2025 executive research also shows why proof matters: AI investment is rising while ROI is frequently difficult to measure and can take longer than normal technology payback expectations.

This supports Hyperion's Evidence Gate, Tool Olympics and Verified Cost Per Outcome thesis.

## 13. Fastest payer thesis

If OTHRYS wants the fastest path to low-touch commercial proof after Course Mode unlocks, Hyperion should investigate in this order:

1. **Developer/AI engineering utilities** — users already pay for tools and understand the value immediately.
2. **Professional productivity / solo operator products** — time saved translates directly into money.
3. **Specific business scans/reports delivered self-serve** — fixed input, automated analysis, downloadable result.
4. **Workflow/template packs** — one-time or subscription bundle; cheap to support.
5. **Buddy premium layers** where free use has created memory and recurring dependency.
6. **Data/intelligence subscriptions** built from Tool Olympics/Prometheus/benchmarks.
7. **SMB operational SaaS** only when onboarding can remain self-serve.
8. **Enterprise control plane/governance** after OTHRYS has strong internal evidence and can justify higher-touch selling.

## 14. Upgrade detector — proposed Hyperion primitive

Every OTHRYS product should eventually emit non-invasive commercial-readiness signals such as:

- active days/week;
- repeated completion of core value event;
- free limit collisions;
- amount of stored useful memory/data;
- repeated manual actions that could be automated;
- exports/downloads;
- number of connected files/projects;
- team/shared-use attempts;
- cost or time saved;
- user returning to same workflow;
- demand for verification/privacy/history/integration.

Do not auto-monetize. Feed these signals to Hyperion so it can estimate **upgrade readiness** while the Product Gate Protocol remains authoritative.

Possible state model:

`CURIOUS -> ACTIVATED -> REPEAT_USER -> DEPENDENT -> UPGRADE_READY -> GATE_ELIGIBLE`

Popularity alone remains insufficient.

## 15. Research caveats

- Consumer payment data is much thinner than usage data; public reports often measure adoption more reliably than actual conversion by age.
- Menlo's market estimates are modeled estimates, useful directionally rather than accounting truth.
- U.S. adoption data should not be copied mechanically to Belgium; Deloitte Belgium provides local confirmation that adoption is already broad.
- Enterprise willingness to pay can be huge, but this can conflict with the user's low-interaction preference because procurement, security and integration increase sales/support burden.
- Hyperion should therefore score **revenue potential minus interaction burden**, not revenue alone.

## New Hyperion scoring modifier

Add:

`LowTouchCommercialScore = Value + Frequency + AbilityToPay + SpeedToProof + SelfServeFit + Reuse + Recurrence - SupportBurden - IntegrationBurden - SalesCycle - ComplianceBurden`

This modifier should be used when ranking opportunities for the user's preferred business model.
