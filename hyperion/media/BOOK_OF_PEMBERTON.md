## Property bible, Season One rough book, and animation production reference

**Status:** PROPERTY DEVELOPMENT BOOK — canonical only for the Pemberton property after operator approval; grants no OTHRYS execution authority.  
**Repository role:** property-specific media canon and production reference.  
**House authority:** current OTHRYS authority remains with the existing Books, Hyperion Arms, Trust Canal, Hephaestus, Talos, Mnemosyne, Switchyard, Keymaster and other admitted systems.  
**Important reconciliation:** this Book does not create a new OTHRYS Titan, does not create a second story system, and does not replace Story Forge or Media Factory.

> **PEMBERTON IS A CAT FIRST, A GENTLEMAN EXPLORER SECOND.**

---

The repo already contains:

# 1. PROPERTY PREMISE

Pemberton is a chubby older tuxedo cat living in the present day who mentally occupies the world of a late-nineteenth-century gentleman explorer.

He is:
- posh;
- educated;
- fastidious;
- vain;
- lazy;
- curious;
- territorial;
- completely fearless around conventional physical danger;
- extremely feline in all practical behavior.

He believes the modern household exists as a service establishment for his benefit.

He does not think he has an owner.

He has **Staff**.

The truth is warmer:

Staff is family.

The comedy works because Pemberton never has to understand that in human language for the audience to understand it emotionally.

---

# 2. ERA AND MINDSET

Pemberton lives now.

His mental and aesthetic model is roughly:
- Belle Époque;
- Victorian/Edwardian gentleman explorer;
- 1870s-to-early-1900s travel culture;
- museum cabinets;
- expedition journals;
- porcelain;
- maps;
- brass;
- old luggage;
- railway/steamship manners;
- overconfident colonial-era travel prose, stripped of actual colonial advocacy and rewritten as harmless domestic pomposity.

He interprets:
- smart-home devices as staff departments;
- modern transport as service infrastructure;
- museums as private collections badly displayed;
- hotels as temporary residences;
- researchers as local specialists;
- tourists as the general public;
- queues as administrative failure;
- warnings as overly cautious paperwork.

He is not historically trapped. He simply believes modernity has made some useful improvements while losing standards.

---

# 3. FIRST CHARACTER LAW — HE IS NOT A SMALL HUMAN

Pemberton does not solve problems with human anatomy.

Avoid:
- habitual upright walking;
- manipulating keyboards like hands;
- sword fighting;
- pointing like a person;
- carrying objects in two paws while walking;
- long face-to-face human conversations;
- using tools in ways a cat physically could not.

Prefer real feline mechanics:
- jumping;
- balancing;
- squeezing;
- climbing;
- hooking with one claw;
- pulling with teeth;
- batting;
- pushing objects from edges;
- sitting on controls;
- following motion;
- hearing subtle sounds;
- smelling;
- fitting through narrow gaps;
- waiting;
- stalking;
- sprinting;
- hiding inside containers;
- occupying the most inconvenient possible surface.

A successful Pemberton solution should often create the reaction:

> **YES. A CAT WOULD ACTUALLY DO THAT.**

---

# 4. FEAR AND DANGER

Pemberton does not display normal human fear of heights.

He can:
- sit on a parapet hundreds of metres above the ground;
- sleep on the edge of a ruin;
- walk across a beam;
- look down into an abyss;
- jump between surfaces with complete confidence;
- occupy ledges that make every human nearby panic.

He is not performing courage.

He simply does not categorize the situation the same way.

This creates a major visual signature:

## THE WORLD-EDGE NAP

After significant effort, Pemberton occasionally chooses an objectively terrifying location and sleeps.

Examples:
- a Colosseum wall;
- a Great Wall battlement;
- a Machu Picchu terrace;
- a high desert stone;
- an Antarctic equipment case beside a crevasse.

The gag only works if used sparingly.

Pemberton may ignore mortal-looking danger while being catastrophically startled by:
- a bag moving unexpectedly;
- a tray dropping;
- a sudden bark;
- a cucumber-like object;
- a spray bottle;
- unexpected water;
- a vacuum maneuver he did not authorize.

> **HEIGHT IS NOT DANGER. LOSS OF DIGNITY IS DANGER.**

---

# 5. LAZINESS

Pemberton is profoundly lazy.

A key joke is that he will invest more energy avoiding effort than the original effort required.

He:
- waits for doors;
- rides luggage;
- sits in baskets;
- occupies carts;
- rides the robot vacuum;
- expects lifts;
- accepts being carried;
- chooses shade;
- finds the warmest surface;
- pauses investigations for naps.

But feline energy is nonlinear.

He can go from stillness to absurd speed because of:
- prey movement;
- food;
- territorial intrusion;
- a dangling object;
- unexplained motion;
- genuine curiosity;
- threat to home.

Then he returns to inactivity.

This contrast is more important than conventional hero stamina.

---

# 6. HUMANS

Modern humans are usually framed from cat height.

Prefer:
- legs;
- shoes;
- hands;
- laps;
- partial torsos;
- voices;
- silhouettes.

Do not make human faces the visual center unless story necessity requires it.

This preserves:
- cat POV;
- Pemberton's importance;
- universality;
- the owner as emotional rather than celebrity focus.

Humans hear cat vocalizations.

The audience may hear Pemberton's translated internal narration.

Humans never hear that narration.

---

# 7. STAFF — THE OWNER

The owner is loving, innocent and ordinary.

Pemberton refers to the owner as **Staff**.

Staff:
- opens doors;
- provides food;
- cleans;
- gives affection;
- repairs things;
- handles transport;
- performs the Spa;
- occasionally shouts "NO!" when Pemberton is being a cat.

Pemberton never concludes Staff is cruel.

If punishment equipment appears, he blames the equipment.

This is particularly important for the **Infernal Atomizer**.

Emotionally:

Pemberton believes he is independent.

The audience sees that he is securely attached to his person.

Never force a sentimental speech.

Show it through behavior:
- he sleeps touching Staff;
- he tracks Staff leaving;
- he returns closer after frightening events;
- he accepts affection even while pretending it is a service;
- he relaxes completely only at home.

---

# 8. HUMAN COOTIES

Pemberton is fastidious about direct human contamination.

He may:
- sit on a napkin instead of a bare café table;
- place a cloth barrier before lying on a public bench;
- avoid unknown hands;
- sniff suspiciously;
- recoil from sticky children;
- use luggage, paper or fabric as an intermediary surface.

He behaves as though humans are biologically questionable.

This rule collapses instantly under the right chin scratch.

Whiskers forward indicate genuine pleasure/affection.

That contradiction is the joke.

---

# 9. THE INFERNAL ATOMIZER

The household owns a skull-shaped spray bottle.

Pemberton considers it a supernatural enemy.

Canonical name in Pemberton's internal language:

# THE INFERNAL ATOMIZER

If Staff uses it while saying "NO!", Pemberton does not blame Staff.

He interprets Staff as temporarily under the Atomizer's influence or forced into service.

The Atomizer may recur as a nemesis but should not dominate the adventure series.

It is strongest as a domestic recurring gag.

---

# 10. COLA RITUAL

Pemberton has a recurring cola-like ritual.

Rights rule:
- no Coca-Cola branding;
- no copied trade dress;
- use a fictional/generic cat-safe cola equivalent.

Home presentation:
- porcelain teacup or small elegant vessel;
- absurd seriousness;
- ceremonial pause.

Field presentation:
- tiny takeaway-style cup or expedition vessel.

The ritual is less about drink and more about Pemberton's belief in civilized standards.

Do not overuse it.

---

# 11. PEMBERTON HOUSE

Pemberton House is the stable home base.

It should feel:
- modern in infrastructure;
- old-world in Pemberton's interpretation;
- safe;
- warm;
- highly automated;
- visually reusable.

Recurring locations:
- living room / Headquarters;
- sofa;
- window;
- food station / Catering;
- litter system / Sanitation;
- Motor Carriage dock / Carriage House;
- Spa/lap zone;
- Grand Tour Door;
- storage/collection area.

Home is a reusable production asset and the emotional anchor of the property.

# 13. GRAND TOUR DOOR

The property already established **Pemberton House** as a teleport base using the **Grand Tour Door**.

Season One must not invent a second unrelated teleport mechanic.

Therefore:

> **THE GRAND TOUR DOOR IS THE PORTAL. THE ATLAS OF SEVEN DOORS IS THE SEASON-ONE KEY/NAVIGATION SYSTEM.**

The Atlas docks with, awakens, or controls the Grand Tour Door.

This reconciles the earlier generic **Lost Atlas** story gene with the already-defined Pemberton House teleport hub.

Season One therefore does not replace the Grand Tour Door.

It reveals what the Door can really do.

---

# 14. THE ATLAS OF SEVEN DOORS

A brass object acquired innocently by Staff at a flea market.

Appearance:
- astrolabe;
- compass;
- puzzle box;
- seven outer seals;
- concealed central/eighth position.

Function:
- interfaces with the Grand Tour Door;
- identifies seven continental routes;
- activates through recovered seals/clues;
- adapts the Motor Carriage for expedition conditions;
- ultimately reveals the hidden Eighth Door.

Important naming boundary:
- this fictional Atlas must never be confused in technical files with **OTHRYS Atlas**, the derived read-only knowledge/system map.

Use technical identifiers like:

`property.pemberton.atlas-seven-doors`

not merely `atlas`.

# 15. MOTOR CARRIAGE / CHARIOT

The robot vacuum is an essential recurring character-device.

At home Pemberton calls it:

# MOTOR CARRIAGE

During expeditions, its transformed configuration may be referred to in production language as:

# CHARIOT

Pemberton does not need to consciously rename it every time.

The audience should understand it is the same beloved cleaning robot.

It does not talk.

It does not need cartoon eyes.

Its personality is generated through:
- route choice;
- docking;
- cleaning obsession;
- literal navigation;
- collision avoidance;
- battery behavior;
- response to Pemberton.

Pemberton believes Motor Carriage exists to transport him.

Motor Carriage believes, to the extent it believes anything, that dirt must be removed.

---

# 16. CHARIOT TRANSFORMATIONS

The Atlas can adapt the Motor Carriage after it crosses the Grand Tour Door.

Season One establishes a bounded set.

## C0 — HOME

Normal robot vacuum.

## C1 — TERRAIN

Articulated wheel/track geometry for stone, stairs and rough ground.

## C2 — GLIDER

Short-range lift/rotor vanes for controlled gliding and vertical assistance.

Not unlimited free flight.

## C3 — SKIFF

Sealed buoyant water configuration.

Pemberton occupies the highest dry point.

## C4 — SLED

Low-friction cold-weather configuration with protected central riding zone.

The system may expand in future seasons only when story need justifies it.

---

# 17. CHARIOT LAW

Chariot solves **mobility**.

Pemberton solves **the mystery**.

Chariot must never become an all-purpose answer.

Limitations:
- battery;
- terrain;
- literalism;
- cleaning priorities;
- docking behavior;
- inability to interpret motives;
- inability to perform historical reasoning;
- physical size.

Sometimes Chariot chooses debris over Pemberton.

This is correct.

---

# 18. PEMBERTON + CHARIOT RELATIONSHIP

It begins as entitlement.

Pemberton sees Chariot as infrastructure.

Across Season One it becomes companionship.

Evidence:
- coordination improves;
- Pemberton learns Chariot's behavior;
- Chariot's collision avoidance occasionally looks suspiciously like concern;
- Pemberton waits beside it when damaged;
- after repair he immediately resumes complaining.

Neither becomes sentimental.

That restraint is important.

# 19. SPEECH AND PERFORMANCE

Pemberton aloud:
- mrrp;
- mrow;
- chirp;
- purr;
- hiss;
- grumble;
- silence.

Internal translated narration:
- articulate;
- formal;
- dry;
- self-important;
- concise.

Example:

Reality:
A donkey ignores him.

Pemberton:
> "Local transport negotiations have stalled."

Reality:
Food dispenser empty.

Pemberton:

Do not over-narrate visual jokes.

# 20. CORE MANNERISMS

Stillness.

One ear rotates.

Blink.

Decision.

---

## DIGNITY RESET

Impact, fall, splash or humiliation.

Sit.

One lick.

Possibly collar/bow adjustment.

Proceed as though intentional.

---

## ROYAL SIT

Front paws aligned.

Tail wrapped.

Used for demands and judgment.

---

## STAFF SUMMONS

"Mrrp."

"Mrrrp."

Wait.

"MRRP."

---

## PAW TEST

Unknown object.

Tap.

Pause.

Tap harder.

Consequences.

---

## BOX LAW

If a container approximately fits Pemberton, Pemberton attempts occupancy.

Historical importance is irrelevant.

---

## OBJECT DISPOSAL

Object occupies desired surface.

Push.

Pause.

Push.

Object falls.

Observe.

No remorse.

---

## EXCITEMENT CHIRP

Something genuinely fascinating appears.

Tiny involuntary chirp.

Immediate composure recovery.

---

## ZOOMIES

Rare and explosive.

Best used when his usual aristocratic inertia has been firmly established.

# 21. SPA

The Spa is Pemberton in his true comfort zone receiving affection from Staff.

It is deliberately disarming.

Possible actions:
- chin scratches;
- cheek rub;
- forehead rub;
- ear rub;
- brushing;
- warm towel;
- paw massage;
- toe-bean rub;
- lap;
- blanket;
- chest scratches.

It is not a literal luxury spa.

It is an affectionate domestic ritual interpreted by Pemberton as rehabilitation services.

---

# 22. SPA LEVELS

## LEVEL 1 — PROFESSIONAL

Half-closed eyes.

Narration still coherent.

## LEVEL 2 — COMPROMISED

Purr.

Paw flex.

Slower narration.

## LEVEL 3 — STRUCTURAL FAILURE

Toe beans spread.

Head rolls back.

Kneading begins.

## LEVEL 4 — COMPLETE SHUTDOWN

Tiny tongue.

Loose paws.

Narration collapses.

## LEVEL 5 — KITTEN

Rare.

Tiny involuntary "mrrp."

Reserved for major emotional payoff.

Season One finale may reach Level 5.

# 23. EMOTIONAL RED LINE

Season One begins with:

> **HOME EXISTS TO SERVE ME.**

The audience gradually sees:

> **HOME IS WHERE I AM SAFE.**

Pemberton never needs to articulate the change.

The Spa, Staff and Chariot carry it.

---

# 24. COMEDY ENGINE

The child often laughs at what happens.

The adult often laughs at Pemberton's interpretation.

Examples:

Child:
Chariot goes the wrong way.

Adult:
Pemberton classifies it as a transport-service dispute.

Child:
Food dispenser is empty.

Adult:
Pemberton launches an internal performance review.

Child:
Medieval or archaeological facilities are rough.

Adult:
Pemberton reviews them like a dissatisfied luxury traveler.

The double layer must never require sexual humor, cruelty or adult content.

---

# 25. THREE-GAG RULE

Every episode should deliberately contain at least:

1. one Pemberton character gag;
2. one location/world gag;
3. one recurring callback;

plus a Spa payoff.

More jokes are allowed.

These four create consistency without joke soup.

# 27. SEASON ONE — THE SEVEN DOORS

**Episodes:** 10  
**Primary arc:** Atlas of Seven Doors  
**Portal:** Grand Tour Door  
**Opposition:** The Collector  
**Companion arc:** Motor Carriage -> Chariot -> genuine team  
**Emotional arc:** Staff as servants -> Staff as home  
**Structure:** pilot + seven continental doors + home convergence + finale

Seven continental routes provide the visual spine.

They are not required to reproduce any commercial "Seven Wonders" list.

The principle is:

> **SEVEN CONTINENTS. SEVEN EXTRAORDINARY PLACES. SEVEN SEALS. ONE HIDDEN EIGHTH DOOR.**

# 28. SEASON BACKSTORY

Long before Pemberton lived there, the house belonged to an eccentric cartographer/inventor connected to the Grand Tour Door.

The Atlas was the navigation and qualification device.

The workshop beneath the house was sealed.

The Atlas disappeared when the estate changed hands.

Years later Staff unknowingly buys it at a flea market.

The Atlas returns home.

The Collector has spent years seeking it.

Pemberton activates it first.

# 42. ATTENTION CONTRACT

Do not build frantic "ADHD editing."

Optimize:
- instant legibility;
- curiosity;
- meaningful motion;
- regular state change;
- clear objectives.

Pemberton production heuristics:

## FIRST IMAGE
Must be comprehensible without context.

## BY ~8 SECONDS
The viewer has a visual question.

## BY ~30 SECONDS
The immediate episode problem is understandable.

## ROUGHLY EVERY 6–12 SECONDS
A meaningful perceptual event normally occurs:
- movement;
- reaction;
- reveal;
- visual change;
- joke;
- object behavior.

This does not require a cut.

## ROUGHLY EVERY 25–40 SECONDS
Deliver:
- clue;
- obstacle;
- payoff;
- answer;
- joke;
- reversal.

## ROUGHLY EVERY 60–90 SECONDS
The story state materially changes.

These are testable production heuristics, not universal psychology laws.

Real retention data later evaluates them.

# 56. AUDIO / SPEECH DEVELOPMENT CANDIDATE

**Status:** CURRENT DEVELOPMENT HYPOTHESIS — not yet promoted to permanent character canon.

The preferred production experiment is now **visual-first near-mute Pemberton**.

This means:
- Pemberton never speaks human language aloud;
- normal performance uses cat vocalizations, breathing, purrs, chirps, hisses and silence;
- internal translated narration is no longer assumed as the default;
- Staff may use sparse natural speech;
- plot comprehension must survive without narration;
- sophistication should come from staging, timing, props, music, visual irony and recurring behavior.

The existing statement that the audience *may* hear translated internal narration remains technically possible during development, but it should be treated as an **exception candidate**, not required infrastructure.

Before permanent canon change, compare the same pilot scene in two versions:

1. near-mute;
2. sparse internal narration.

Evaluate:
- comprehension;
- humor;
- charm;
- sophistication;
- replay value;
- international readability;
- production burden.

Current expectation:

> **NEAR-MUTE SHOULD WIN UNLESS NARRATION CONTRIBUTES MEANING THAT THE VISUAL STORY CANNOT CARRY CLEANLY.**
