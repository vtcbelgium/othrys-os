## Mission

Turn the House of Crius from a catalogue of rooms into a living hunt. Every candidate gets examined for payer, pain, distribution, existing OTHRYS reuse, revenue routes, indirect/grey-research routes, moat, cheapest proof, blockers, and kill conditions.

CRIUS is hungry for **legitimate capture of value**, including non-obvious paths. Grey means research required, never permission.

## Evidence pulse — September 2026

Current external research strengthens two OTHRYS theses:

1. **Agent proof/evaluation is a real pain surface.** Production-agent evaluation remains fragmented across tracing, behavioral evaluation, regression, security and governance. Real-world browsing agents still show material gaps on transactions, multi-step execution and safeguards. This favors OTHRYS concepts built around Evidence Gate, Execution Receipts, qualification, Tool Olympics and task-specific reliability rather than another generic agent wrapper.
2. **Education AI is enormous but crowded; learning proof is the wedge.** OECD 2026 reports GenAI use is mainstream among many student populations while warning that task completion does not equal learning. Purpose-built pedagogical use performs better than cognitive offloading. Study Buddy therefore should compete on *proved learning, recall, gaps and source grounding*, not generic chat.

External evidence is research input only. It does not promote a product or open a gate.

# DOSSIER 001 — TOOL OLYMPICS / BUILDER ARENA

## Why hunt it
OTHRYS already needs comparative qualification of models, builders and tools. That means the cost of producing the core dataset can partially overlap with internal engineering work.

## Payers
- engineering teams choosing agents/models;
- AI consultancies;
- enterprises doing procurement;
- vendors wanting independent testing;
- developers consuming public comparisons;
- media/research consumers;
- API customers needing routing intelligence.

## Revenue surfaces
- free public leaderboard;
- premium detailed benchmark reports;
- historical data subscription;
- benchmark API;
- custom workload benchmarking;
- vendor qualification fee where payment buys testing, never outcome;
- disclosed sponsorship of benchmark seasons/categories;
- disclosed affiliate/referral links after independent scoring;
- newsletter sponsorship;
- enterprise procurement report;
- licensing charts/data/methodology;
- paid alerts when a model regresses or a cheaper model overtakes it.

## Grey-research surfaces
- vendor-funded testing with strict independence firewall;
- bounty/prize programs for reproducible failures;
- revenue-share procurement referrals;
- derived aggregate performance intelligence.

## Moat
Longitudinal controlled evidence: task -> environment -> model -> cost -> latency -> attempt -> failure -> recovery -> verified result.

## Cheapest proof
Take 5 stable OTHRYS tasks, run 5 available builders/models under identical contracts, publish an internal scorecard. Check whether results materially change routing decisions.

## Kill conditions
No reproducibility; tests cannot compare fairly; maintenance cost overwhelms information value; results are mostly obvious/provider marketing duplicates.

# DOSSIER 004 — AI BILL / STACK OPTIMIZER

## Thesis
AI buyers increasingly face a routing problem, not merely a model-selection problem: quality, cost, latency, privacy, reliability and task fit vary.

## Free wedge
Upload or enter workload characteristics -> receive estimated model/provider alternatives and potential savings.

## Paid layers
- workload replay;
- continuous spend anomaly monitoring;
- model regression alerts;
- routing policy;
- Switchyard integration;
- enterprise cost dashboard;
- procurement report;
- implementation/shared-savings contract where legally and contractually appropriate.

## Indirect revenue
Disclosed provider referrals can coexist with independent recommendations only if commission never enters the quality score.

## Moat
Tool Olympics feeds actual task-performance data into the optimizer. The optimizer feeds workloads back into benchmark design.

## Cheapest proof
Replay a known OTHRYS workload against 3–5 models and calculate cost/quality frontier.

## Core promise
`INGEST -> UNDERSTAND -> RETRIEVE -> PRACTISE -> TEST -> FIND GAP -> RETEST -> PROVE MASTERY`

## Differentiators
- source-grounded answers;
- no-answer mode when evidence is absent;
- active recall;
- spaced repetition;
- gap finder;
- mastery graph;
- exam simulation;
- confidence-vs-performance calibration;
- teacher/course provenance;
- evidence that the learner can answer without AI.

## Revenue later
Student Pro, course packs, school licences, white-label training provider, teacher dashboard, institutional analytics with appropriate privacy, affiliate learning resources where useful.

## Grey-research surfaces
Institution-sponsored access, training-provider revenue share, grants/education innovation funding, bundled licences.

## Cheapest proof
Use the operator's upcoming course as a year-long dogfood dataset. Measure actual quiz retention and gap closure rather than chatbot engagement.

# CRIUS HUNGER RULE

For every valuable OTHRYS asset, CRIUS must be able to answer:

> **What are the ten credible ways this could create economic value, who captures that value today, and what evidence would justify OTHRYS capturing some of it later?**

If CRIUS answers only `subscription`, the House scan is incomplete.

If CRIUS answers `we can probably make money somehow`, the evidence is incomplete.

If CRIUS proposes deception, concealment, user exploitation, unlawful access, or bypassing the financial lock, the proposal is rejected.

Hungry means exhaustive.

Hungry does not mean reckless.
