# HYPERION ANTI-SLIP CHECKER

> Purpose: prevent Hyperion and OTHRYS from drifting into sunk-cost loyalty, vanity work, support traps, margin traps, stale products, or commercially weak maintenance obligations.

The Anti-Slip Checker is a recurring brutal review mechanism.

It judges assets, not people.

It exists because time, money, compute and operator attention are finite.

---

## 1. THE QUESTION

For every product, website, tool, pack, dataset, service, API, Buddy, experiment or commercial branch:

> **IF THIS DID NOT EXIST TODAY, WOULD WE CHOOSE TO SPEND THE NEXT HOUR, EURO, TOKEN, DEPLOYMENT SLOT OR SUPPORT MINUTE ON IT?**

If the answer is no, the burden of proof shifts to the asset.

---

## 2. REQUIRED INPUTS

Where measurable:
- monthly direct revenue;
- monthly variable cost;
- monthly infrastructure cost;
- monthly support minutes;
- monthly maintenance minutes;
- operator interventions;
- active users;
- repeat users;
- retention;
- core-value completion;
- organic acquisition;
- paid acquisition if any;
- refund/complaint rate;
- reliability/failure rate;
- security/compliance burden;
- data/traffic/SEO value;
- strategic learning value;
- cross-product distribution value;
- resale/licensing value;
- technical debt trajectory;
- dependency/platform risk;
- replacement availability;

UNKNOWN is recorded as UNKNOWN, never silently converted into GOOD.

---

## 3. BRUTAL SCORE

Suggested score:

`SURVIVAL_SCORE = CASH_VALUE + STRATEGIC_VALUE + TRAFFIC_VALUE + DATA_VALUE + LEARNING_VALUE + OPTIONALITY + REUSE - RUN_COST - SUPPORT_BURDEN - MAINTENANCE_BURDEN - RISK - COMPLEXITY - OPERATOR_ATTENTION - OPPORTUNITY_COST`

Scores are relative, not absolute accounting truth.

The point is forced comparison.

---

## 4. RED FLAGS

Immediate review triggers:
- support burden rising faster than usage;
- recurring manual rescue;
- negative or unknown unit economics after enough data exists;
- no repeat usage;
- no organic acquisition after reasonable exposure;
- vanity traffic with no core-value completion;
- infrastructure cost > total strategic value;
- product requires operator memory to function;
- repeated broken dependencies;
- regulatory/platform burden exceeds plausible upside;
- incumbent or open-source alternative has clearly surpassed us;
- customer-specific exceptions accumulating;
- one noisy user consumes disproportionate time;
- product blocks more valuable work;
- maintenance backlog growing faster than capability;
- commercial model relies on tricks rather than value;
- feature set grows while retention stays flat;
- AI/model cost rises without outcome improvement;
- product exists mainly because we already spent time on it.

---

## 5. SMALL-ASSET EXCEPTION

Do not automatically kill low-revenue assets.

A EUR 5/month website can survive when:
- direct costs are lower;
- maintenance is nearly automatic;
- security exposure is bounded;
- no meaningful support exists;
- it provides useful traffic/data/learning;
- it may compound slowly;
- it consumes essentially no operator attention.

The correct comparison is not `EUR 5 is tiny`.

It is:

`TOTAL VALUE > TOTAL BURDEN ? KEEP : REVIEW`

---

## 6. ACTION STATES

Every review ends in one of:

`EXPAND` — strong evidence; invest more.

`KEEP` — healthy asset; current allocation justified.

`AUTOMATE` — value exists but human burden must be removed.

`SIMPLIFY` — remove features/dependencies/complexity.

`REPRICE` — economics weak because price/value capture mismatch.

`BUNDLE` — standalone economics weak but combined value strong.

`KEEP_FREE` — strategic distribution/data/trust value exceeds direct monetization value.

`OPEN_SOURCE` — external maintenance/distribution value may exceed private ownership value.

`LICENSE` — retain IP while reducing operating burden.

`SELL` — asset has transferable value but no longer deserves OTHRYS attention.

`ARCHIVE` — preserve knowledge/code; stop live burden.

`KILL` — shut down cleanly.

No asset gets `KEEP` by default merely because nobody reviewed it.

---

## 7. ANTI-SUNK-COST LAW

Past investment is historical data, not a claim on future capital.

Do not ask:
- how much did we spend already?

Ask:
- what does the next unit of attention buy?

Past work matters only insofar as it created reusable assets, evidence, distribution, users, rights, data, reputation or strategic options.

---

## 8. ANTI-VANITY LAW

Hyperion discounts:
- impressions without intent;
- traffic without activation;
- downloads without successful use;
- registrations without retention;
- social mentions without qualified demand;
- stars without product dependency;
- praise without payment intent or strategic value;
- technical sophistication without user value.

A boring tool that saves 10 minutes every day can beat an impressive autonomous demo nobody needs.

---

## 9. ANTI-SUPPORT-TRAP LAW

Human support is treated as a cost center and product-design signal.

Repeated support issue ->
`DOCS -> PREFLIGHT -> VALIDATOR -> SELF-TEST -> AUTO-DIAGNOSIS -> PRODUCT FIX`

If the same explanation is given twice, consider encoding it.

If the same repair is done twice, consider automating it.

If a product cannot become reasonably self-serve, Hyperion sharply discounts it under the operator's low-touch strategy.

---

## 10. COMPETITIVE REVIEW

Periodically ask:
- is there now a better competitor?
- can we replace a paid dependency?
- can a cheaper model/provider achieve the same verified result?
- has a platform commoditized our feature?
- has user frustration shifted?
- is there a newly obvious distribution channel?
- can our own second product cannibalize the first more efficiently?

Hyperion should be willing to cannibalize OTHRYS products before competitors do.

---

## 11. GREY-EDGE REVIEW

Aggressiveness is allowed in research and business-model design.

Before deployment, any boundary-sensitive tactic must pass:
- legal basis where relevant;
- platform/provider terms;
- rights/licensing;
- truthful representation;
- clear sponsorship/affiliate disclosure;
- privacy/consent;
- trust impact.

If the economic advantage disappears once disclosed honestly, reject it.

If the tactic only works by violating terms or confusing users, reject it.

If it is unusual but lawful, transparent and valuable, investigate aggressively.

---

## 12. REVIEW CADENCE

Recommended future automation:
- new experiment: review quickly after first meaningful evidence;
- active small assets: monthly or quarterly depending volatility;
- high-cost/high-support products: more frequently;
- infrastructure dependencies: on material pricing/reliability change;
- strategic winners: continuous telemetry plus scheduled portfolio review.

Cadence is proportional to downside and rate of change.

---

## 13. KILL CHECKLIST

Before termination:
- export useful data;
- preserve reusable code/knowledge;
- measure whether redirects are valuable;
- protect users from surprise loss where commitments exist;
- disable billing/revenue safely;
- cancel unnecessary infrastructure;
- archive evidence of why it died;
- feed lesson back to Mnem/Hyperion;
- check whether asset can be sold/licensed/open-sourced instead.

Dead products should still teach the factory.

---

## 14. CORE LAWS

> **NO LOYALTY TO SUNK COST.**

> **THE NEXT HOUR MUST EARN ITS PLACE.**

> **SMALL CASHFLOW MAY LIVE; LARGE BURDEN MAY DIE.**

> **AUTOMATE REPEATED SUPPORT OR COUNT IT AS PRODUCT DEBT.**

> **CANNIBALIZE YOURSELF BEFORE THE MARKET DOES.**

> **IF THE EDGE REQUIRES DECEPTION, IT IS NOT AN EDGE.**

> **KEEP WHAT COMPOUNDS. KILL WHAT CONSUMES.**
