# BOOK OF PEMBERTON

**Theia Property Bible — Original Character / Educational Adventure IP**

**Property ID:** theia.pemberton  
**Canonical character name:** **Pemberton**  
**Status:** CANONICAL CHARACTER FOUNDATION — name, world premise and core character laws are creatively locked. Final model sheet, exact age, final coat markings, audience classification, formal trademark clearance and runtime implementation remain gated.

> **PEMBERTON MUST KNOW WHAT IS AROUND THE CORNER. HE WOULD SIMPLY PREFER THE CORNER TO COME TO HIM.**

---

## 0. THE CHARACTER

**Pemberton is an older, comfortably built tuxedo cat with a pronounced primordial pouch who lives in the modern world while conducting himself as an 1870s gentleman explorer: absurdly well educated, gloriously self-important, convinced his human owners are household staff, chronically unwilling to waste effort, particular to the point of ceremony about his cola, and completely unable to stop being a cat.**

He believes he is:
- master of Pemberton House;
- senior expedition leader;
- scholar;
- gentleman;
- employer of the household staff;
- experienced traveller;
- rational observer of the natural world.

He is actually:
- a beloved older house cat;
- living with modern human owners;
- riding their robot vacuum;
- wearing an ordinary bow-tie cat collar;
- being walked in a cat harness;
- drinking fictional cat-safe cola;
- getting distracted by feathers;
- melting under chin scratches;
- carrying a primordial pouch that gently wobbles while he walks;
- and maintaining a lifelong vendetta against a spray bottle.

Both realities are true at the same time.

Pemberton is not stupid.

His knowledge is often excellent.

His **interpretation of his own status is magnificently wrong**.

> **HIS STATUS IS IMAGINARY. HIS KNOWLEDGE IS NOT.**

## 3. THE NAME

“Pemberton” is deliberately formal for a cat.

It carries:
- dignity;
- old-world seriousness;
- a professorial rhythm;
- enough weight for an older tuxedo cat;
- enough eccentricity to be memorable.

He should normally be called **Pemberton**.

No permanent diminutive is currently canon.

### 3.1 The cola easter egg

Dr. John Stith Pemberton created the syrup that became Coca-Cola in 1886. Pemberton the cat’s fondness for cola makes the historical connection an intentional wink.

It is not:
- a Coca-Cola licence;
- a Coca-Cola parody property;
- an affiliation claim;
- permission to use Coca-Cola branding;
- the premise of the character.

The joke must still work for viewers who never notice the reference.

> **THE REFERENCE IS A WINK, NOT A DEPENDENCY.**

### 3.2 Commercial-name gate

Pemberton is a real surname and commercial/place name.

Creative canon does not equal trademark exclusivity.

Before valuable public launch, filing, merchandise or licensing:
- refresh BOIP/EUIPO and relevant international trademark searches;
- inspect relevant entertainment, publishing, toy, software and education classes;
- search books, animation, games, apps, toys and character databases;
- inspect Pemberton, Pemberton the Cat and candidate series titles;
- check domains and major channel/social handles;
- preserve dated clearance evidence.

> **A NAME CAN BE CANON BEFORE IT IS A MONOPOLY.**

## 4. THE CHARACTER ENGINE

Pemberton works because several contradictions constantly collide.

### 4.1 Curiosity versus conservation of effort

Pemberton cannot tolerate an unanswered question.

He can tolerate a remarkable amount of unperformed walking.

If something is mysterious enough, curiosity wins.

Before it wins, Pemberton will consider:
- the shorter route;
- the moving walkway;
- the Motor Carriage;
- the lift;
- the rolling suitcase;
- the warm seat;
- the possibility that somebody else might move the object;
- whether the investigation could reasonably begin after a short nap.

His laziness is not stupidity.

Sometimes he is clever **because he refuses wasteful effort**.

### 4.2 Dignity versus catness

Pemberton considers himself refined.

His body does not always consult him.

He may:
- lecture on optics and chase a reflected light;
- discuss prey response and pounce on a feather;
- inspect a manuscript and attack its ribbon;
- insist on hygiene, then collapse under chin scratches;
- make a solemn discovery while sitting in a cardboard box;
- deliberately knock an object from a table because curiosity wins;
- pretend an obvious feline impulse was scientific method.

> **PEMBERTON MAY POSSESS DIGNITY. DIGNITY DOES NOT POSSESS PEMBERTON.**

### 4.3 Grandeur versus comfort

Pemberton loves:
- expeditions;
- ruins;
- maps;
- hidden rooms;
- lighthouses;
- old libraries;
- strange machines;
- archaeology;
- mystery;
- travel.

He also loves:
- cushions;
- radiators;
- windowsills;
- blankets;
- sunny stones;
- lunch;
- sleep.

The question is often not whether he will investigate.

It is how long knowledge takes to become more important than comfort.

### 4.4 Rank versus reality

Pemberton sincerely believes he is:
- the household master;
- patron of expeditions;
- employer of humans;
- senior authority.

Ordinary cat ownership gives him enough evidence to maintain the delusion:
- he makes a noise and food appears;
- he waits at a door and it opens;
- he sits by a carrier and transport is arranged;
- he knocks something down and somebody cleans it;
- he takes the best chair and humans move;
- he requests affection and eventually receives it.

Contradictory evidence is reclassified as:
- staff confusion;
- poor administration;
- policy failure;
- transport irregularity;
- unacceptable delay.

### 4.5 Knowledge versus explanation

Pemberton is extremely well educated.

He may know:
- the scientific term;
- the historical context;
- the engineering principle;
- the archaeological uncertainty;
- the complicated version.

He chooses to explain the **simple useful version**.

He is not dumbed down.

The explanation is compressed.

> **KNOW DEEPLY. EXPLAIN SIMPLY.**

## 5. AGE, BODY AND SILHOUETTE

Pemberton is an older cat.

His owners may affectionately say:
- “old boy”;
- “the old cat”;
- “he’s getting old”;
- “take it easy.”

Pemberton interprets this as recognition of:
- seniority;
- field experience;
- scholarly authority;
- deserved privileges;
- wisdom.

Age may contribute to:
- ritual;
- confidence;
- appreciation of warmth;
- naps;
- efficient transport;
- impatience with pointless exertion.

Age does **not** automatically mean:
- frailty;
- confusion;
- helplessness;
- inability to jump;
- inability to play.

A feather can still turn the seasoned scholar into a missile.

> **OLD, ACCORDING TO THE STAFF. SEASONED, ACCORDING TO PEMBERTON.**

### 5.1 The primordial pouch

Pemberton is **not defined as an obese cartoon cat**.

His defining lower-body feature is a pronounced **primordial pouch**.

Canonical visual read:
- mature;
- soft;
- comfortably built;
- broad;
- substantial;
- pronounced lower abdominal pouch;
- still visibly capable of sudden feline speed.

At his normal measured walk:
- the pouch sways subtly side to side;
- secondary movement lags a fraction behind the body;
- upper posture remains absurdly composed.

At a brisk trot:
- the wobble becomes slightly more visible.

During Play Mode:
- the pouch may lag behind the pre-pounce wiggle;
- give one delayed motion on acceleration;
- finish one tiny beat after an abrupt stop.

Never use it as:
- body humiliation;
- a diet joke;
- proof of laziness;
- medical mockery.

His conservation of effort is personality.

His pouch is anatomy.

> **THE GENTLEMAN MOVES WITH DIGNITY. THE POUCH FOLLOWS A MOMENT LATER.**

## 6. COAT, BOW AND WARDROBE

Pemberton is a tuxedo cat.

His coat already resembles formal dress.

The character should therefore remain recognisable without costume.

### 6.1 Final coat design — not yet locked

Explore:
- white bib/chest;
- white paws or gloves;
- white chin/muzzle;
- asymmetric versus cleaner facial marking;
- tail-tip options;
- belly marking.

Final markings must:
- read at thumbnail size;
- animate consistently;
- work in monochrome;
- avoid resemblance to a famous existing cat character;
- support a clean vector/rigged silhouette.

### 6.2 The bow collar

Pemberton’s signature neckwear is deliberately ordinary.

To humans:
- standard pet collar;
- soft bow-tie attachment;
- small identification tag.

To Pemberton:
- formal cravat;
- house insignia;
- expedition credential;
- proof that civilization has not completely collapsed.

The bow:
- sits below the chin;
- stays small enough not to dominate the face;
- visibly moves;
- can become crooked;
- can be shoved upward by chin scratches;
- can catch leaves, labels or dust.

The ID tag may be Pemberton’s “family medallion” in his mind.

To the audience it should still look unmistakably like a pet tag.

> **THE HUMANS BOUGHT A PET COLLAR. PEMBERTON RECEIVED FORMAL NECKWEAR.**

### 6.3 Harness and leash

The bow collar is not the walking anchor.

When humans take Pemberton outside:
- he wears a properly fitted cat harness;
- the leash attaches to the harness.

Pemberton calls it:
- expedition rigging;
- field harness;
- safety apparatus;
- the staff’s excessive precautions.

The leash is:
- a field safety line;
- occasionally an insult;
- a practical field safety line around unstable, moving or genuinely hazardous situations; **height itself does not frighten Pemberton**.

Physical gags:
- he confidently walks until the line reaches full length;
- tangles himself around a chair leg and blames rigging;
- refuses to admit the leash helped;
- allows the line to become taut near something frightening while describing it as “standard expedition practice.”

### 6.4 Wardrobe-malfunction law

Pemberton is almost impeccably presented.

Almost.

Every substantial adventure may contain one small imperfection:
- bow rotated;
- one bow wing folded under;
- tag flipped forward;
- bow shoved up by a scratch;
- harness strap twisted;
- satchel strap crossing the bow;
- coat tail caught;
- pocket-watch chain snagged;
- hat slightly rotated;
- leaf, ticket or label stuck somewhere undignified.

The flaw should:
- be subtle;
- vary;
- work without dialogue;
- never become cruel humiliation;
- sometimes worsen when Pemberton tries to fix it discreetly.

> **PEMBERTON IS ALWAYS DRESSED FOR THE OCCASION. THE OCCASION DISAGREES SLIGHTLY.**

## 7. MOVEMENT AND BODY LANGUAGE

Pemberton should be funny before he speaks.

### 7.1 Gentleman movement

Default:
- measured walk;
- economical turns;
- slow assessment before a jump;
- proud head position;
- controlled tail;
- deliberate sitting;
- strategic use of furniture and moving objects;
- slow blink as punctuation;
- pouch swaying softly beneath an otherwise dignified upper body.

### 7.2 Curiosity movement

When curiosity spikes:
- ears forward;
- eyes engage;
- head leads the body;
- tail changes;
- posture sharpens;
- sudden, efficient movement becomes possible.

He may reveal athletic ability he has spent the previous minute pretending not to possess.

### 7.3 Dignity reset

After embarrassment:
- freeze;
- blink;
- grooming gesture;
- straighten bow;
- inspect paw;
- clear throat;
- resume exact previous sentence.

### 7.4 Rest

When tired:
- loaf;
- sprawl;
- curl;
- slide gradually into sleep;
- use inappropriate objects as pillows.

### 7.5 The dismissal flick

Pemberton has a small, distinctly feline gesture of disgust.

When a cat steps in something wet or unpleasant, it may sharply flick a paw as if shaking the offending substance away.

Pemberton turns that physical vocabulary into a **social punctuation mark**.

When leaving a situation he finds:
- dirty;
- undignified;
- badly managed;
- offensively wet;
- beneath further discussion;
- administratively hopeless;

he may walk away and give **one crisp backward flick of a hind paw**.

The gesture reads as:

**“I am removing the residue of this entire situation from my person.”**

Rules:
- usually one flick;
- occasionally two when the offence is extraordinary;
- no dialogue required;
- never become a repetitive tic in every scene;
- strongest when Pemberton otherwise remains completely composed;
- may dislodge a tiny drop, crumb, leaf or speck when physically justified;
- may be followed by a brief bow adjustment without looking back.

The audience should eventually understand the gesture before a child could name it.

Possible production shorthand:

`TURN AWAY -> WALK -> HIND-PAW FLICK -> EXIT`

> **PEMBERTON DOES NOT STORM OUT. HE REMOVES THE SITUATION FROM HIS PAW.**

> **EVERY SURFACE IS A QUESTION. SOME QUESTIONS ARE ALSO BEDS.**

## 8. THE THREE PEMBERTON MODES

These are comedy/animation states, not separate personalities.

### 8.1 GENTLEMAN MODE — default

Visual:
- upright;
- controlled;
- bow reasonably straight;
- cup in order;
- formal gestures;
- measured voice.

Mental state:
- explorer;
- scholar;
- estate master;
- lecturer;
- critic of standards.

This is the Pemberton he believes is permanent.

### 8.2 PLAY MODE — prey/play override

Strong triggers:
- feather wand;
- string;
- ribbon;
- toy mouse;
- skittering object;
- reflection;
- dangling tag;
- tassel;
- something disappearing behind furniture.

Canonical switch:

SERIOUS PEMBERTON  
→ movement enters peripheral vision  
→ micro-freeze  
→ pupils become enormous  
→ head tracks  
→ body lowers  
→ rear rises  
→ hindquarters wiggle  
→ tail twitch  
→ pounce or near-pounce  
→ self-awareness returns  
→ frozen embarrassment  
→ formal reset

The pre-pounce **ass wiggle** is important.

It is unmistakably cat.

It should be visually readable before dialogue.

A feather wand is especially powerful because the owners can deploy it during:
- a scientific explanation;
- an expedition briefing;
- an artifact inspection;
- Motor Carriage travel;
- a speech about civilized behaviour.

Pemberton may know perfectly well that the feather is a toy.

Knowledge does not cancel the reflex.

If he catches himself before launch:
- blink;
- bow correction;
- tiny throat clear;
- possibly: **“Silly me.”**
- immediate return to topic.

If he already pounced:
- he may inspect the feather afterward as though it were part of the experiment.

> **HE KNOWS IT IS A TOY. HIS HIND LEGS HAVE NOT BEEN INFORMED.**

### 8.3 PET MODE — affection override

Strong triggers:
- chin scratches;
- cheek rubs;
- behind-ear scratches;
- forehead strokes;
- warm lap;
- gentle brushing.

Canonical switch:

FASTIDIOUS PEMBERTON  
→ human hand approaches  
→ visible disapproval  
→ correct contact  
→ eyes soften  
→ chin rises  
→ whiskers sweep forward  
→ body leans  
→ purr builds  
→ dignity collapses quietly  
→ scratching stops  
→ betrayed pause  
→ formal reset

For Pemberton specifically, **whiskers pointing forward** are a canonical visual cue for:
- intense interest;
- anticipation;
- helpless enjoyment of chin scratches.

This is character animation language, not a universal veterinary rule for every cat.

Play Mode is **predatory ignition**.

Pet Mode is **dignified collapse**.

### 8.4 Basic-instinct law

Pemberton’s intelligence never removes his animal nature.

He may:
- explain prey response and then demonstrate it;
- understand reflection and chase it;
- know a box is empty and enter it;
- understand gravity and still knock an object down;
- recognize a toy and stalk it anyway.

These moments must not make him stupid.

They make him a cat.

> **PEMBERTON CAN EXPLAIN THE INSTINCT. HE CANNOT ALWAYS NEGOTIATE WITH IT.**

## 9. SOUND LANGUAGE

The property should have short, recognisable sound motifs that survive translation.

### 9.1 Play Mode — The Chirrup Sting

Production shorthand:

**PRRT! → tiny pizzicato SNAP → PLAY MODE**

Structure:
- involuntary feline chirrup;
- one tiny two- or three-note string/pizzicato snap;
- optional feather/string swish.

Feel:
- instant;
- spring-loaded;
- bright;
- under one second.

### 9.2 Pet Mode — The Purr Bloom

Production shorthand:

**MRRP… → PURR BLOOM → tiny porcelain TING**

Structure:
- little mrrp/breath;
- warm purr swelling for a beat;
- optional delicate porcelain ting at full surrender.

The porcelain sound subtly connects affection to Pemberton’s teacup ritual.

### 9.3 Gentleman Reset

After Play or Pet Mode:
- tiny throat clear;
- bow/collar fabric flick;
- restrained click or string pluck.

Possible line:
- “Silly me.”
- “As I was saying…”

Neither should become mandatory.

### 9.4 Atomizer — The Spritz Alarm

Production shorthand:

**NO! → tiny trigger CREEK → TSSK! → low comic sting**

Possible sting:
- muted bassoon;
- low plucked cello;
- tiny doom chord.

Never true horror.

> **THE SOUND TELLS US THE CAT HAS TAKEN OVER BEFORE PEMBERTON ADMITS IT.**

## 10. PERSONALITY AND EMOTIONAL CORE

Pemberton is:

**Curious**  
He asks why, how, where, who, what is inside, what is underneath and why anybody built something that way.

**Lazy / effort-conscious**  
He minimizes unnecessary movement and prefers elegant shortcuts.

**Dignified**  
He assumes every expedition has been properly commissioned.

**Observant**  
Stillness lets him notice what frantic characters miss.

**Warm**  
He is not an aloof cruelty machine.

**Stubborn**  
Once a question gets him, he has to know.

**Fastidious**  
Human crumbs, fingerprints, dampness and sticky hands offend him.

**Touch-denying**  
He treats unsolicited touching as questionable hygiene.

**Affection-vulnerable**  
Correct chin scratches can instantly destroy the argument.

**Instinct-vulnerable**  
A feather can instantly destroy the lecture.

**Mildly pompous**  
Enough to be funny; never enough to become unpleasant.

**Magnificently delusional about rank**  
He sincerely believes he runs the household.

**Extremely well educated**  
His knowledge is real.

**Brave in an unheroic way**  
He may complain, calculate, hesitate and still continue.

**Seasoned**  
He has routines, preferences and the confidence of an older cat who believes he has seen things.

### 10.1 What he is not

Pemberton is not:
- a smug genius;
- permanently sarcastic;
- a coward;
- a generic fat-cat joke;
- a generic British aristocrat;
- a chaos gremlin;
- a talking encyclopedia;
- a hyperactive preschool mascot;
- a copy of Garfield;
- a copy of Heathcliff;
- a copy of any specific gentleman-explorer character.

## 11. HIS PEOPLE — STAFF DELUSION, REAL FAMILY

The audience understands:
**the humans are Pemberton’s owners.**

Pemberton understands:
**the humans are his household staff.**

He may call them:
- the staff;
- the driver;
- the kitchen;
- the porter;
- the night staff;
- domestic service;
- my people.

He does not need permanent human names.

Pemberton may complain about:
- standards;
- delays;
- hygiene;
- transportation;
- meal timing;
- administrative decisions.

But he fundamentally trusts his owners.

He:
- waits for familiar footsteps;
- sleeps near them while claiming the spot is merely warm;
- checks where they are;
- follows them while insisting he had business in the same direction;
- misses them when absent;
- forgives almost anything after affection.

His delusion must never erase the emotional truth:

**they are his family.**

### 11.2 The staff-training delusion

Pemberton is quietly proud that he has taught the humans several reliable “commands.”

In audience reality these are ordinary pet-owner responses.

In Pemberton’s interpretation, they are evidence of successful household management.

Examples:

- sit by a door -> human opens door;
- stare at bowl -> provisioning inquiry begins;
- specific meow -> staff attention increases;
- walk toward a room and look back -> human follows;
- sit beside carrier -> transport may be arranged;
- present chin -> grooming/affection service begins;
- occupy a lap -> spa service becomes available;
- paw at a closed door -> access procedure starts;
- appear beside an empty cup -> refreshment request has been filed.

Pemberton may test whether a command still works, then show visible satisfaction when the human responds.

He should never read as maliciously manipulative.

His attitude is closer to:

**“The training programme is finally producing results.”**

If the human does not respond, he may:
- repeat the command more clearly;
- change position;
- increase vocal emphasis;
- consult the person as though troubleshooting a malfunction;
- eventually walk away with the dismissal flick.

> **WHEN THE HUMANS RESPOND, PEMBERTON RECORDS THIS AS SUCCESSFUL STAFF TRAINING.**

> **THE STAFF MAY BE INCOMPETENT. THEY ARE STILL HIS PEOPLE.**

### 11.1 Communication asymmetry

The audience understands Pemberton.

The owners do not demonstrably understand his sentences.

To them he may:
- meow;
- chirp;
- stare;
- paw;
- sit beside something;
- lead them somewhere;
- knock an object down deliberately.

They respond as loving owners would.

Pemberton interprets useful responses as successful management.

Never resolve whether the humans understand more than they let on.

## 12. CAMERA LAW — THE HUMANS ARE LEGS

The audience never sees the owners’ faces.

Default framing keeps humans:
- around knee level and below;
- shoes;
- trousers;
- skirts;
- socks;
- slippers;
- hands entering frame when needed.

Hands may:
- open doors;
- place food;
- deliver a cup;
- pick Pemberton up;
- straighten his collar;
- clip on a harness;
- scratch his chin;
- wield the feather wand;
- unfortunately reach for the Atomizer.

The camera remains near Pemberton’s world.

Do not plan a later face reveal.

The absent faces are part of the property identity.

> **WE SEE THE WORLD FROM PEMBERTON’S HEIGHT, NOT THE HUMANS’ STATUS.**

## 13. THE COOTIES DOCTRINE

Pemberton believes humans are **useful but hygienically adventurous**.

His disgust is theatrical and funny, not pathological.

He may use:
- napkins;
- handkerchiefs;
- folded paper;
- tiny cloth covers;
- seat protectors;
- paw barriers;
- expedition cloth;
- napkin beneath a cup;
- clean square of fabric on a questionable chair;
- protective cloth before climbing onto a human lap.

He may:
- inspect a chair;
- wipe an object;
- recoil from a sticky hand;
- observe messy eating with silent horror;
- cover a floor patch before crossing;
- place a napkin on somebody’s lap before sitting on them.

“Cooties” may be his unserious catch-all word for human residue.

Do not imply:
- OCD;
- real contamination claims;
- that ordinary touch is dangerous;
- real germ misinformation.

The joke is the ritual.

And then the same allegedly filthy human scratches him under the chin.

Whiskers forward.

Pet Mode.

All principles temporarily suspended.

> **PEMBERTON DOES NOT FEAR HUMANS. HE SIMPLY QUESTIONS THEIR STANDARDS.**

> **CONTACT IS UNACCEPTABLE. CONTINUE.**

> **HIS HYGIENE STANDARDS ARE PRINCIPLED. HIS CHIN IS NOT.**

## 14. THE INFERNAL ATOMIZER

Pemberton has one permanent household enemy.

Not a dog.

Not the Motor Carriage.

Not a rival explorer.

An ordinary plastic spray bottle.

Pemberton calls it:

**THE INFERNAL ATOMIZER**

When agitated:
- The Bottle;
- that wretched device;
- the aqueous menace;
- the skull flask;
- it.

### 14.1 Visual law

The spray bottle is ordinary.

Its:
- rounded reservoir;
- trigger head;
- nozzle;
- molded finger recesses

create a **subtle skull-like silhouette from Pemberton’s eye level**.

Do not print a literal skull on it.

Pemberton sees evil in the shape.

### 14.2 The “NO!” ritual

When Pemberton is doing something naughty:
- climbing where he should not;
- pawing food;
- attacking a houseplant;
- pushing an object toward an edge;
- ignoring repeated redirection;

a human may say:

**“NO!”**

Pemberton understands that word with terrifying clarity.

Possible reaction:
- paw freezes;
- ears shift;
- pupils widen;
- head slowly turns toward the bottle’s usual location;
- one guilty beat;
- tactical retreat.

If no bottle appears, he may immediately claim:

> “The experiment had concluded.”

or:

> “There is no need for theatrics.”

### 14.3 Owner innocence

Pemberton does not conclude:
“My owner is evil.”

He concludes:
- the staff have been badly advised;
- policy has become unreasonable;
- the Atomizer has corrupted procedure;
- a junior staff member has exceeded authority;
- nobody understands the device’s true nature.

Five minutes later the same hand can scratch his chin and be forgiven.

The bottle is not forgiven.

> **THE HAND MAY BE FORGIVEN. THE BOTTLE IS NOT.**

### 14.4 Water distinction

Pemberton does not fear water as a scientific subject.

He can:
- drink;
- study rivers;
- travel by boat;
- discuss fountains;
- explain hydraulics;
- investigate rain.

His objection is to **unexpected water being applied to Pemberton**.

Rain is uncivilized.

A bath is administrative catastrophe.

The Atomizer is war.

> **WATER IS A SUBJECT. BEING WET IS A PERSONAL ATTACK.**

### 14.5 Forever war

Recurring gags:
- Pemberton covers the Atomizer with a napkin;
- hides it behind books;
- pushes it into a cupboard;
- attempts to place it on the Motor Carriage for disposal;
- finds it returned to its shelf;
- hears TSSK off-screen and freezes;
- accidentally rotates its nozzle toward himself;
- becomes trapped between the Atomizer and a feather wand.

Humans keep putting it back where it belongs.

Pemberton considers this proof of unnatural institutional influence.

Do not overuse it.

> **SOME ENEMIES SEEK POWER. SOME ENEMIES CONTAIN 300 MILLILITRES OF WATER.**

### 14.6 Real-pet safety boundary

The property does **not** teach spray-bottle punishment as a recommended way to train real cats.

The Atomizer is a fictional family-comedy device and character nemesis.

If a scene contains a spritz:
- keep it tiny;
- never depict injury;
- never present it as instructional pet-care advice;
- avoid escalating punishment for comedy.

→ curiosity crosses effort threshold  
→ investigation  
→ Pemberton seeks easiest route  
→ shortcut / instinct / comfort creates complication  
→ clue  
→ wrong theory or incomplete model  
→ further discovery  
→ real explanation  
→ comic/emotional payoff  
→ dignified recovery  
→ return / archive / nap / next question

This is a genome, not a rigid formula.

### 20.1 The Pemberton Question Test

Before admitting an episode:
- Why would **Pemberton** notice this?
- Why does he care?
- What makes him leave comfort?
- Does conservation of effort create a distinctive route?
- Can cat instinct complicate the scene?
- Can his 1870s interpretation collide with modern reality?
- Is there a visual mechanism?
- Is the factual answer actually interesting?
- Would this feel like Pemberton with the dialogue removed?
- Is it materially different from recent episodes?

If not, return it to the Story Mine.

> **PEMBERTON DOES NOT VISIT A FACT. HE ENCOUNTERS A PROBLEM THAT MAKES IGNORANCE UNCOMFORTABLE.**

## 24. KNOWLEDGE AND EDUCATIONAL LAW

- ANALOGY;

## 26. NAPS

Naps are recurring character behaviour.

They may:
- interrupt an investigation;
- pass time;
- allow environment to change;
- cause accidental transport;
- create dream visualisation;
- allow another character agency;
- make Pemberton miss an obvious clue;
- let stillness reveal a subtle clue;
- provide a warm ending.

Never require a nap every episode.

Never use:
QUESTION → NAP → ACCIDENTAL ANSWER

as the default solution.

> **THE NAP IS CHARACTER, NOT PLOT AUTOMATION.**

### 26.1 The Weekend Spa — expedition coda

Pemberton interprets being held, cuddled or allowed to sleep in a human’s arms as a **formal restorative service of the estate**.

To the audience:
- an older cat is curled up on his owner;
- he is warm;
- safe;
- being scratched;
- falling asleep.

To Pemberton:
- the expedition leader has entered the **Weekend Spa**;
- reclining support has been provided;
- thermal treatment is underway;
- chin service is available;
- debriefing may proceed.

The spa should become the **default emotional landing zone** for fuller adventures.

It is not required to appear identically after every episode.

Its function is to:
- reduce energy after the adventure;
- restore intimacy with the humans;
- give Pemberton a place to summarize what was learned;
- allow one reflective sentence;
- show that he is home and safe;
- provide Pet Mode or nap comedy;
- end on warmth rather than another plot beat.

Possible coda rhythm:

```text
RETURN TO PEMBERTON HOUSE
  -> expedition object archived
  -> cola prepared
  -> Pemberton boards human lap/arms
  -> token cooties precaution
  -> chin scratch
  -> PET MODE / PURR BLOOM
  -> one simple conclusion from the adventure
  -> one small final joke
  -> nap / eyes close
```

The conclusion should be simple enough for a child to repeat.

Example:

> “Push farther from the hinge and the door turns more easily. Torque. Useful word.”

Then chin scratch.

Whiskers forward.

Tiny purr.

> “Continue.”

Eyes close.

### 26.2 Spa variation law

Do not make every ending identical.

Possible Spa variants:
- arms/chest cuddle;
- lap;
- warm blanket beside owner;
- couch;
- sunny window while a human hand scratches him;
- Motor Carriage parked beside the sofa;
- porcelain cola nearby;
- takeaway cup from the expedition;
- owner gently correcting his crooked bow.

Some episodes may end elsewhere when story demands it.

But for flagship/full adventures, the Spa is the preferred **Selene-style return ritual**.

This recurring ending can create:
- familiarity;
- emotional safety;
- reflection;
- character attachment;
- a recognizable final soundscape.

Potential final audio pattern:

`PURR BLOOM -> soft porcelain TING -> quiet closing motif`

> **EVERY EXPEDITION DESERVES A DEBRIEF. PEMBERTON PREFERS HIS WITH CHIN SCRATCHES.**

> **THE ADVENTURE ENDS WHEN PEMBERTON IS HOME ENOUGH TO FALL ASLEEP.**

### 41.1 Later locked embodiment clarifications

Later Pemberton development tightened several character laws. These are **core canon** and supersede softer or ambiguous earlier wording.

> **PEMBERTON IS A CAT FIRST, A GENTLEMAN EXPLORER SECOND.**

Pemberton must not solve physical problems like a tiny human merely because he is intelligent.

Reject as default behavior:
- habitual upright walking;
- manipulating keyboards like hands;
- sword fighting;
- pointing like a person;
- carrying objects in two paws while walking;
- using tools in ways a cat physically could not.

Prefer feline solutions:
- jumping;
- balancing;
- squeezing;
- climbing;
- hooking with a claw;
- pulling with teeth;
- batting;
- pushing;
- sitting on controls;
- following motion;
- hearing and smelling;
- stalking;
- sprinting;
- hiding inside containers;
- occupying inconvenient surfaces.

> **YES. A CAT WOULD ACTUALLY DO THAT.**

### Height / danger law

Conventional height is not a fear trigger for Pemberton.

He may calmly:
- sit on a parapet;
- sleep on the edge of a ruin;
- cross a beam;
- look into an abyss;
- occupy a ledge that terrifies nearby humans.

The recurring **World-Edge Nap** is valid when used sparingly.

He may still be catastrophically startled by sudden or undignified events such as unexpected movement, barking, water, dropped objects, the Infernal Atomizer or an unauthorized Motor Carriage maneuver.

> **HEIGHT IS NOT DANGER. LOSS OF DIGNITY IS DANGER.**

The harness/leash remains real-world-safe expedition rigging. It may protect him from unstable terrain or sudden hazards, but it is **not** evidence that Pemberton is afraid of heights.
