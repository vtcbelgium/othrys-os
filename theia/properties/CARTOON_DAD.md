# PRACTICAL CARTOON DAD — THEIA STARTER PROPERTY

**Status:** UTILITY-FIRST PROPERTY SEED — working identity only; final name/character design not locked.

## 1. Proven job

There is demonstrated demand for warm, practical "dad-style" instruction:
- tie a tie;
- shave/iron clothes;
- unclog a drain;
- use tools;
- hang a shelf;
- check oil/tire pressure;
- jump-start a car;
- basic household maintenance;
- confidence around ordinary adult tasks.

Theia may independently serve this job.

Theia must not clone "Dad, How Do I?" or any other creator's:
- name;
- character/likeness;
- scripts;
- catchphrases;
- jokes;
- episode edits;
- thumbnails;
- branding.

> **COPY THE JOB. BUILD A DIFFERENT FATHER.**

## 2. Promise

A funny, competent cartoon dad calmly shows the viewer how ordinary practical things work.

Tone:
- warm;
- encouraging;
- slightly corny;
- never condescending;
- safety-conscious;
- competent without macho posturing.

## 3. Why cartoon

Benefits:
- reusable character/IP;
- no filming location required;
- exact diagrams/cutaways;
- localization;
- repeatable production;
- easy shorts/long-form derivatives;
- privacy;
- B2B/white-label potential.

Risks:
- unsafe oversimplification;
- generic templating;
- lack of tactile realism;
- repetitive scenes.

The visual must add explanation, not only decoration.

D. Car/basic mobility

E. Life competence

## 5. Safety boundary

Do not automate dangerous instruction casually.

High-risk categories:
- mains electrical work;
- gas;
- structural work;
- roofing/heights;
- pressure systems;
- chemicals;
- automotive operations with severe injury risk.

For those:
- explain principles/safety;
- route to official/manual/professional help;
- avoid pretending a cartoon is sufficient certification.

Country/product differences must be stated when material.

## 9. Episode inventory and risk tiers

The first public batch should deliberately avoid high-consequence instructions. We want to learn the media machine before asking it to teach tasks where one bad frame can hurt someone.

### Tier A — preferred first pilots

1. Tie a simple necktie knot.
2. Sew a button back on.
3. Iron a shirt without creating new creases.
4. Pick the right screwdriver bit.
5. Use a tape measure correctly.
6. Use a spirit level.
7. Read common screw-head types.
8. Build a sensible first-apartment tool kit.
9. Explain what common pliers/wrenches are for.
10. Replace simple removable furniture/cabinet hardware.

These are visually clear, useful, low-consequence and excellent tests of animated hands, close-ups, arrows and step sequencing.

### Tier B — after the instructional QA system proves itself

11. Choose a wall plug/anchor conceptually.
12. Unclog a simple accessible sink trap safely.
13. Understand why a toilet keeps running.
14. Check tire pressure.
15. Read basic tire sidewall information.
16. Replace windshield wipers.
17. Check engine oil using the specific vehicle/manual procedure.
18. Locate common water shutoff concepts.
19. Bleed a radiator only for explicitly supported system types.
20. Drill/screw basics on known test material.

### Tier C — explain principles / route to authoritative procedure, not generic autopilot

- jump-starting vehicles;
- mains electrical work/reset diagnosis beyond a simple user-safe breaker action;
- gas;
- structural work;
- roofing/heights;
- high-pressure systems;
- hazardous chemicals;
- model-specific automotive repair.

Tier C can still make useful videos, but the goal is often "understand the situation and know when/how to use the manual or professional", not "follow this generic cartoon and perform the repair."

## 10. Pilot

**First five recommended tests:**
1. tie a tie;
2. sew a button;
3. choose the correct screwdriver bit;
4. use a tape measure;
5. use a spirit level.

These give five different visual/instruction problems without introducing electrical, gas, structural or vehicle-starting risk.

Start with tasks that are:
- visually explainable;
- low risk;
- frequently searched;
- international or easy to localize;
- cheap to animate;
- easy to verify against authoritative references or ordinary physical reality.

Measure:
- search impressions;
- completion;
- saves;
- comments saying task succeeded;
- repeat viewership;
- production time;
- correction rate.

## 11. Anti-copy doctrine

Study:
- user need;
- query language;
- category demand;
- common confusion;
- teaching mechanics.

Do not reproduce:
- another creator's emotional backstory as branding;
- distinctive father persona;
- script language;
- channel identity.

Theia should create its own reason to exist.

## 12. Final laws

> **PRACTICAL CONFIDENCE IS THE PRODUCT. THE CARTOON IS THE DELIVERY SYSTEM.**

> **IF THE VIEWER CANNOT DO THE TASK MORE SAFELY AFTER WATCHING, THE VIDEO FAILED.**

> **WARMTH IS PART OF THE FORMAT; ACCURACY IS PART OF THE CONTRACT.**
