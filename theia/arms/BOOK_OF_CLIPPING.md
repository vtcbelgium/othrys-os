# BOOK OF THEIA — CLIPPING & REPURPOSING ARM

**Purpose:** lawfully transform source media into high-value native derivatives with very low operator effort.

> **CLIP VALUE, NOT JUST VIDEO.**

## 1. Source classes

Preferred:
- OTHRYS-owned video/audio;
- creator/client footage with explicit repurposing rights;
- campaign-supplied footage under explicit licence;
- public-domain or compatible licensed media;
- long-form media produced specifically for a derivative system.

Blocked by default:
- downloaded creator footage without permission;
- film/TV/sports/music clips merely because they are popular;
- source whose music/visual rights do not cover derivatives;
- ambiguous campaign terms;
- material obtained by platform-term evasion.

## 2. Pipeline

    RIGHTS
    -> INGEST
    -> TRANSCRIBE
    -> DIARIZE / SEGMENT
    -> CANDIDATE FIND
    -> CONTEXT BOUNDARY
    -> SCORE
    -> CUT
    -> REFRAME
    -> CAPTION
    -> OPTIONAL BRANDING
    -> QA
    -> REVIEW
    -> PACKAGE
    -> PUBLISH IF AUTHORIZED
    -> MEASURE

## 3. Candidate scoring

Score candidate moments on:
- standalone comprehensibility;
- truthful hook strength;
- information/emotion density;
- clear beginning;
- payoff;
- target duration fit;
- visual suitability;
- speaker continuity;
- audience fit;
- novelty vs other candidates;
- rights/policy risk.

Penalize:
- mid-sentence openings;
- context dependence;
- misleading edits;
- repeated point;
- dead air;
- unsafe claim isolation;
- sponsor/ad segment confusion.

## 4. Context integrity

Clipping must not change meaning through omission.

Sensitive claims may require:
- preceding qualifier;
- following correction;
- on-screen context;
- rejection as a standalone clip.

## 5. Automation target

The optimal first automation target is not zero humans.

Preferred:

    AI creates 5–15 candidates
      -> automated quality filter
      -> operator sees top 2–5
      -> approve/reject/repair
      -> automated package; publish only under declared authority

Measure:
- operator seconds/minutes per published clip;
- repair rate;
- false-positive candidate rate.

The eventual goal is exception-based review for low-risk proven workflows.

## 6. Monetization paths

### A. Own-content repurposing
Safest, highest rights clarity.

### B. Authorized creator/podcast service
Strong recurring low-touch model:
- source arrives;
- agreed number of derivatives;
- approval queue;
- delivery/publishing;
- monthly retainer or package.

### C. Licensed campaign clipping
Good low-friction experiment:
- no creator acquisition required when marketplace/campaign supplies rights and terms;
- campaign terms, caps, allowed platforms and payment rules must be recorded per run;
- record whether the licence permits only campaign posting, broader portfolio display, account monetization, archive/reuse or none of those;
- never fake views.

### D. B2B internal repurposing
Webinars, training, product demos, events, founder content.

### E. Owned distribution
Clips feed Theia/OTHRYS properties; money may come later through Hyperion channels.

Do not base the business on unlicensed repost farms.

## 7. Economics

Track:
- source minutes processed;
- transcription cost;
- candidate generation cost;
- rendering cost;
- operator review time;
- clips approved;
- clips published;
- revenue attributable by contract/campaign;
- cost per approved clip;
- operator attention per clip.

A clipping workflow that needs 15 minutes of repair per short is not yet low-touch.

## 8. Provider strategy

Early proof may use clipping SaaS.
Long-term local/control path may combine:
- fast transcription;
- word timestamps;
- diarization;
- LLM candidate selection;
- FFmpeg-class cutting/rendering;
- speaker/face tracking;
- subtitle rendering;
- official publishing APIs.

Current research examples include OpusClip/Vizard-class SaaS and WhisperX/faster-whisper/pyannote/FFmpeg-class local components. They are adapters, not architecture.

## 9. Platform/reuse gate

There are **four separate questions**:

1. Do we have the legal/licensed right to publish?
2. Does the platform allow the upload?
3. Is the artifact sufficiently original/transformative for the intended platform monetization program?
4. Does the specific campaign/client agreement permit us to monetize or reuse it?

A yes to one is not a yes to the others.

YouTube's current reused-content policy explicitly states that minimal third-party reuse may be ineligible for monetization **even with the original creator's permission**.

Before publishing:
- rights clear;
- licence scope recorded;
- platform allows use;
- reused-content/originality risk assessed separately;
- derivative has genuine value;
- metadata truthful;
- required disclosure/tags present;
- no spam/mass-upload behavior.

> **PERMISSION IS NOT A YPP PASS.**

## 10. Learning

Store:
- source;
- candidate transcript;
- score;
- accepted/rejected;
- rejection reason;
- edit amount;
- platform;
- metrics;
- revenue/cost where legitimate.

Over time the ranker may learn operator/property preferences.

## 11. Final laws

> **CAPTIONS + CROP DO NOT CREATE RIGHTS.**

> **CONTEXT IS PART OF THE CLIP.**

> **THE BEST AUTOMATION REMOVES SEARCH AND EDITING WORK, NOT JUDGMENT BEFORE IT IS EARNED.**
