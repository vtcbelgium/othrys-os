# ROOM 16 — MOATS & STRATEGIC VALUE

Mission: identify value that may be more important than immediate revenue.

## Strategic assets CRIUS tracks

- proprietary execution/failure histories
- benchmark history
- trusted qualification corpus
- provenance-rich project memory
- cross-model routing data
- repair outcomes
- reusable eval suites
- distribution/audience
- integrations/connectors
- domain-specific templates
- credibility around evidence-backed completion
- relationships with users, schools, agencies, vendors or institutions
- open-source adoption
- brand names and recognized standards

## Non-revenue value

An asset can be strategically valuable because it:
- lowers future customer acquisition cost
- improves another product
- creates switching cost through accumulated user value without trapping users
- increases negotiation power with vendors
- attracts partners
- attracts consulting/contracts
- builds credibility for training/employment/institutional relationships
- generates proprietary data
- reduces OTHRYS operating cost
- accelerates future product creation

## Moat test

For every serious product ask:
1. What gets better with every user/run/month?
2. What would a competitor need years rather than weeks to recreate?
3. Does OTHRYS learn something proprietary from operating this?
4. Does distribution compound?
5. Are integrations or workflow depth increasing?
6. Can users export their data? If not, are we relying on hostage lock-in instead of real value?
7. Does the moat survive model commoditization?

## Preferred moat hierarchy

Strong:
- unique outcome data
- verified historical evidence
- workflow integration
- domain evals
- trusted standard/certification
- owned audience
- compounding knowledge

Weak:
- UI alone
- generic prompts
- access to the same foundation model everyone can buy
- novelty naming
- artificial lock-in

## CRIUS rule

Do not optimize every product for immediate cash. Some products should stay free or cheap when they dramatically strengthen distribution, evidence, data, brand, or another higher-value product.

A monetization model that destroys a stronger moat is a bad commercial decision.
