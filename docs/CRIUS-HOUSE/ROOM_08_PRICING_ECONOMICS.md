# ROOM 08 — PRICING & UNIT ECONOMICS

Mission: make CRIUS financially literate before money is ever turned on.

## Questions every commercial candidate must answer

- What is the unit of value?
- What is the unit of cost?
- Which usage pattern creates value?
- Which usage pattern destroys margin?
- Who pays?
- What is the willingness-to-pay evidence?
- What support burden appears at scale?
- What happens if model/API/infrastructure cost doubles?
- What happens if usage becomes 10x or 100x?
- Is pricing easy to explain?
- Is there a natural free-to-paid boundary?

## Pricing models to test

- flat subscription
- per-seat
- per-repo
- per-project
- per-workspace
- usage-based
- credit packs
- per-report
- outcome-based
- shared savings
- licence + maintenance
- setup + recurring retainer
- free individual / paid team
- open-source core / paid hosted
- paid API with free public surface

## Cost model

At minimum track:
- model/API cost
- compute
- storage
- bandwidth
- third-party SaaS
- payment fees
- support time
- manual review time
- refunds/chargebacks
- sales/onboarding effort
- compliance/admin overhead

## Scenario sheet

For every candidate model calculate at least:
`10 USERS | 100 USERS | 1K USERS | 10K USERS | 100K USERS`

For each:
- revenue
- variable cost
- fixed cost
- gross margin
- support load
- infrastructure risk
- likely operational bottleneck

## Pricing research while gates are closed

Allowed:
- competitor price mapping
- willingness-to-pay interviews
- fake-door interest tests that do not take payment and are clearly non-transactional
- waitlists
- packaging surveys
- internal simulations
- sandbox checkout flows
- cost-envelope stress tests

Not allowed under closed financial gate:
- taking real payment
- misleading users into believing a paid service is currently available

## Commercial robustness tests

A candidate should survive:
- 2x API cost
- 50% lower conversion than expected
- high-use customer
- free-tier abuse
- support-heavy cohort
- refund scenario
- provider outage
- replacement of a critical third-party vendor

If the business model only works under perfect assumptions, it is not gate-ready.
