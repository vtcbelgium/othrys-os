# BOOK OF FLIPSETS

> **Status:** PRODUCT CANON / INCUBATOR — MONEY GATE CLOSED
> **Working name:** FlipSets
> **North star:** **Know the flip before you buy.**
> **Primary loop:** `FIND -> CHECK -> BUY? -> TRACK -> SELL -> LEARN`
> **Scope law:** start with one decision, earn the right to become a platform.
> **Commercial law:** development and testing may proceed while the Money Gate is closed; no real monetization, affiliate revenue, paid subscription, sponsored placement, marketplace commission, payment handling, or commercial launch may occur until the gate is explicitly unlocked.

FlipSets is a dedicated LEGO-set flipping decision and reseller workflow product. It is **not** a LEGO encyclopedia, a generic collection tracker, an investment-prediction site, a marketplace, or a reskinned VTC. Its first job is smaller and sharper:

> **A person sees a set at a price. FlipSets tells them what the deal actually looks like after costs, with evidence and uncertainty visible.**

Everything else is earned later.

---

## 1. WHY FLIPSETS EXISTS

LEGO resale attracts several overlapping groups: collectors who occasionally sell, bargain hunters, retirement/EOL investors, used-set restorers, part-out sellers, marketplace resellers, and small professional stores. They all face the same first question:

> **If I buy this at this price, is there realistically money left after I sell it?**

Existing tools often answer adjacent questions very well:

- What is this set worth?
- How has its price moved?
- Is it retiring?
- What are its parts worth?
- What do I own?
- Where can I buy it?

FlipSets begins with a different unit of value: **the deal in front of the user**.

A displayed market price is not profit. MSRP is not cost basis. A current listing is not a sale. A forecast is not cash. A 40% discount can still be a bad flip. A low-margin set that sells in three days can be more useful to a reseller than a supposedly high-growth set that occupies space for two years.

FlipSets therefore models the economics of the transaction rather than merely the prestige or headline value of the set.

## 2. PRODUCT THESIS

### 2.1 The wedge

The initial wedge is deliberately narrow:

`SET + BUY PRICE + MARKET + CONDITION -> REALISTIC NET FLIP VIEW`

The user should be able to reach the answer in seconds while standing in a shop, browsing an online retailer, looking at a marketplace listing, or assessing a local lot.

The first successful FlipSets experience is not:

> “I created my account and imported 800 sets.”

It is:

> “This set is €64.99. I checked it. FlipSets showed that the apparent €35 spread becomes about €12 after realistic costs, so I knew what I was dealing with before spending money.”

That moment is the product.

### 2.2 The long-term product

If the wedge works, FlipSets can grow into a lightweight reseller operating system:

`SOURCE -> EVALUATE -> ACQUIRE -> LOCATE -> AGE -> LIST -> SELL -> RECONCILE -> LEARN`

The system becomes more useful as it observes **the user's own realized transactions**, because the strongest future advantage is not another generic LEGO forecast. It is knowing how *this user* buys and sells:

- their actual marketplace fees;
- their average shipping cost;
- their preferred markets;
- their historical sell-through time;
- their storage constraints;
- their realized discount to headline market value;
- their return rate and cancellation rate;
- the themes and price bands they personally move well;
- their capital locked in slow inventory.

Generic market data becomes personalized operating intelligence.

## 3. WHAT FLIPSETS IS NOT

Scope protection matters because the LEGO ecosystem is enormous.

FlipSets V1 is **not**:

- a Brickset replacement;
- a Rebrickable replacement;
- a BrickLink replacement;
- a BrickEconomy replacement;
- a BrickPicker clone;
- a general MOC platform;
- a universal loose-part inventory manager;
- a social network;
- an auction house;
- a payment intermediary;
- an automated scalping bot;
- a promise of investment returns;
- a tax adviser;
- a fully automated trading system.

Those exclusions are strategic. FlipSets wins first by reducing **decision latency** and making deal economics honest.

## 4. MARKET REALITY — SEPTEMBER 2026

The market is not empty. That is good: demand is proven. It also means weak differentiation will fail.

### BrickPicker

BrickPicker is the strongest direct competitive warning. As of September 2026 it has evolved into a broad LEGO market-intelligence and reseller platform. Its paid plan is listed at **$29.99/month or $299/year** and includes full price history, large portfolios, photo import, storage locations and QR labels, reseller economics, real-time deals, arbitrage, retirement monitoring, eBay sold data, BrickLink part-out detail, receipt OCR, business records, sales ledger, sell advisor, eBay seller tools, Shopify tooling, API access and an MCP server.

Its own positioning explicitly fuses sourcing, inventory, sales and bookkeeping. FlipSets therefore cannot assume that “fee-adjusted arbitrage + inventory” is an uncontested category.

**Lesson:** do not begin by trying to match BrickPicker's breadth. Beat complexity with focus, speed, locality and a superb deal decision.

### BrickEconomy

BrickEconomy is strong in valuation, historical performance, retirement and longer-term investment framing. Its premium positioning emphasizes collection intelligence, forecasts and market timing.

**Lesson:** FlipSets should distinguish **resale execution** from investment storytelling. Forecasts may later be evidence inputs, but the first question is realized economics.

### BrickLink

BrickLink is a core marketplace and pricing reference. Its API price guide can return current stock and last-six-month sales statistics for sets, parts and other catalogue item types, segmented by new/used, country/region and currency.

BrickLink API terms matter commercially. Reasonable commercial use is allowed in general, but the terms prohibit selling access to BrickLink data/services and prohibit charging users specifically for an aspect of an application that integrates an API feature BrickLink itself provides free. Default API volume is documented as 5,000 calls/day, with contact requested for uncertain or higher-volume commercial cases.

**Lesson:** BrickLink is a candidate evidence provider, not a dependency to build the entire paid proposition around without explicit commercial review/permission.

### Rebrickable

Rebrickable provides an API for official set/part/minifigure catalogue data and recommends bulk CSV downloads for full-catalogue ingestion. Its API documentation explicitly states that it does not provide pricing data because pricing belongs to external sites.

**Lesson:** catalogue identity and market pricing should be separate provider layers.

### Scanner apps

Brick Search, BrickScan, Brickify and similar products demonstrate that recognition/scanning is increasingly commodity functionality. Recognition is useful, but it is not sufficient as a moat.

**Lesson:** scanning should remove input friction. The valuable result is the decision that follows recognition.

### Competitive conclusion

FlipSets should not claim a magical data monopoly. Its potential advantage is the combination of:

1. **instant deal economics;**
2. **local/market-correct assumptions;**
3. **transparent evidence and confidence;**
4. **actual realized-sale learning;**
5. **capital and storage efficiency;**
6. **a much simpler mobile workflow than full reseller suites.**

## 5. FIRST CUSTOMER

The first customer archetype is not a hedge-fund-style LEGO investor.

It is the **practical opportunistic reseller**:

- sees discounted sets in stores or online;
- may flip a few sets per month;
- uses eBay, BrickLink, local marketplaces or similar channels;
- currently calculates margins mentally or in a spreadsheet;
- is vulnerable to fees, shipping and slow stock destroying apparent profit;
- does not want a complex business ERP before the first sale;
- wants to know quickly whether a deal deserves more attention.

A second archetype comes later: the **serious reseller** managing dozens/hundreds of units, locations, receipts and multiple sales channels.

A third archetype is the **collector who occasionally flips** duplicates or bargains.

V1 optimizes for the first archetype.

## 6. CORE PRODUCT LAWS

### LAW 1 — TRUE PROFIT, NOT HEADLINE SPREAD

Every deal view prioritizes estimated **net proceeds** and **net profit** over MSRP discounts and gross spread.

### LAW 2 — SOLD EVIDENCE OUTRANKS ASKING PRICES

Evidence hierarchy:

`USER'S REALIZED SALES > VERIFIED SOLD MARKET DATA > MARKETPLACE SALES STATISTICS > CURRENT LISTINGS > RETAIL PRICE > MODEL FORECAST`

A current listing tells us what somebody asks. It does not prove what buyers pay.

### LAW 3 — MARKET AND CONDITION MUST TRAVEL WITH THE PRICE

€120 in the US converted to euros is not automatically Belgian market evidence. New/sealed is not used. Complete used is not incomplete used. Every price record keeps:

- source;
- geography;
- currency;
- condition;
- timestamp;
- sample/count where available;
- whether it is sold, listed, retail, forecast or manually entered.

### LAW 4 — UNCERTAINTY MUST BE VISIBLE

FlipSets may calculate. It may estimate. It may rank evidence. It may never present uncertain future value as guaranteed profit.

### LAW 5 — FEES ARE USER REALITY

Marketplace fees change. Shipping changes. Packaging changes. VAT/tax treatment depends on the user's situation. Fee profiles must therefore be configurable and versioned rather than silently hard-coded forever.

### LAW 6 — CAPITAL HAS A COST

€30 profit after 18 months and €12 profit after two weeks are not equivalent. Holding time and cash velocity matter.

### LAW 7 — SPACE HAS A COST

Large LEGO boxes consume storage. Eventually FlipSets should be able to surface **profit per unit of storage over time**, not just ROI.

### LAW 8 — AI IS AN ACCELERATOR, NOT AN ORACLE

Use barcode/UPC/set-number/catalogue matching before expensive vision. Use AI where ambiguity remains. Never add AI merely to decorate a calculation.

### LAW 9 — DO NOT BUILD THE MARKETPLACE FIRST

Transactions between users create moderation, trust, fraud, payments, disputes and regulatory surface. FlipSets first helps people decide and manage. Selling can happen on existing marketplaces.

### LAW 10 — MONEY GATE CLOSED BY DEFAULT

No code path may accidentally convert the incubator into a commercial service. See §19.

## 7. THE MVP — ONE SCREEN THAT EARNS ITS EXISTENCE

### 7.1 Input

The user provides:

- set number / barcode / search result;
- price they can buy it for;
- condition: sealed / new-open-box / used-complete / used-incomplete;
- intended resale market or saved profile;
- optional quantity.

Everything else should be inferred or defaulted where evidence permits.

### 7.2 Output: the Deal Card

A Deal Card should contain no more information than necessary to decide whether to investigate further.

Example:

```text
FLIPSETS — 75379 R2-D2

BUY                         €64.99
REALISTIC RESALE            €94–€101
EST. SELLING COSTS          €14–€18
EST. NET PROFIT             €11–€21
BREAK-EVEN SALE PRICE       €81
EST. ROI                    17–32%
EVIDENCE                    MEDIUM

Sold evidence: 23 recent observations
Market: EU / sealed
Updated: 3h ago

[ SAVE DEAL ]   [ ADD PURCHASE ]
```

A compact qualitative label may exist — e.g. `STRONG / THIN / NEGATIVE / UNCERTAIN` — but it must be derived transparently from economics and evidence, not a mystical proprietary score.

### 7.3 What V1 does not need

The MVP does **not** need:

- social profiles;
- badges;
- forums;
- complex AI chat;
- retirement forecasting;
- store maps;
- automated listing generation;
- tax returns;
- part-out optimization;
- bulk portfolio forecasting;
- multi-user teams;
- public APIs;
- browser extensions;
- native apps.

A responsive PWA can prove the loop first.

## 8. ECONOMICS ENGINE

The engine exists to prevent false profit.

### 8.1 Base model

Let:

`C_buy` = purchase price

`C_inbound` = shipping/travel attributable to acquisition

`C_prep` = cleaning, replacement pieces, packaging preparation

`C_storage` = optional allocated storage carrying cost

`P_sale` = realistic expected sale price

`F_market` = marketplace fee

`F_payment` = payment-processing fee where separate

`C_ship` = seller-paid outbound shipping

`C_pack` = box, tape, filler, label etc.

Then:

`TOTAL_COST_BASIS = C_buy + C_inbound + C_prep + C_storage`

`NET_SALE_PROCEEDS = P_sale - F_market - F_payment - C_ship - C_pack`

`NET_PROFIT = NET_SALE_PROCEEDS - TOTAL_COST_BASIS`

`ROI = NET_PROFIT / TOTAL_COST_BASIS`

`NET_MARGIN = NET_PROFIT / P_sale`

The calculation layer must keep tax/VAT treatment distinct from simplistic “profit” math. Where the user's commercial/tax profile is unknown, the app should say so rather than imply after-tax profit.

### 8.2 Break-even

Break-even is one of the most useful figures in the entire product.

The app should solve for the sale price at which expected net profit reaches zero given the active fee profile. This is more actionable than a vague market valuation.

### 8.3 Conservative / base / optimistic bands

Where the data supports it, show three scenarios:

- **Conservative:** lower realizable sale price + normal costs;
- **Base:** median/weighted realistic case;
- **Optimistic:** upper but evidence-supported case.

Never let the optimistic scenario become the default headline.

### 8.4 Time

Once enough sale history exists:

`CAPITAL_VELOCITY = realized ROI / days held`

A yearly normalized metric may be displayed carefully, but extremely short holds can create absurd annualized percentages. The UI should prefer intuitive forms such as:

> “€80 tied up for an estimated 24 days.”

### 8.5 Storage

Later:

`SPACE_EFFICIENCY = expected net profit / storage volume / expected holding period`

This is intentionally parked beyond MVP, but the data model should not make it impossible.

## 9. EVIDENCE ENGINE

A deal is only as trustworthy as the evidence behind it.

Every market observation should be represented as evidence, not flattened into an unexplained single number.

Minimum fields:

```text
source
source_item_id
set_id
market
country/region
currency
condition
observation_type = sold | listing | retail | forecast | user_sale
price
shipping
observed_at
sample_size (when available)
confidence/provenance metadata
```

### Confidence grades

**A — strong:** recent sold evidence, correct market/condition, healthy sample.

**B — useful:** sold evidence but smaller/older or broader geography.

**C — indicative:** current listings or sparse data.

**D — weak:** converted foreign-market evidence, model estimate, old data.

**UNKNOWN:** insufficient evidence; do not fabricate a signal.

The user should be able to tap the evidence line and understand why FlipSets produced the range.

## 10. THE BENELUX WEDGE

Current BrickPicker documentation names native deal coverage for the US and initial coverage for Canada, UK, Australia, Germany, France and Italy. Belgium and the Netherlands are not listed as native deal markets at the time of this research.

That is a **possible opening, not yet a moat**.

FlipSets should investigate a Belgium/Netherlands-first market layer:

- EUR-native calculations;
- Belgian and Dutch shipping assumptions;
- retailer feeds where contractually permitted;
- local retailer price histories where legally obtainable;
- local marketplace fee profiles;
- local sold evidence where available legitimately;
- cross-border reality: BE/NL/DE/FR can all matter to a Belgian reseller;
- VAT-aware labels rather than blindly copying US reseller assumptions.

Do not fake locality by converting US prices into euros.

Locality becomes valuable only if the underlying evidence is genuinely local or clearly marked otherwise.

## 11. DATA PROVIDER STRATEGY

### 11.1 Separation law

Never create one provider that becomes synonymous with “truth.”

Keep separate adapters for:

- **catalogue identity**;
- **retail offers**;
- **sold-market evidence**;
- **current listings**;
- **retirement/EOL evidence**;
- **part-out evidence**;
- **images**;
- **user-realized sales**.

### 11.2 Catalogue

Rebrickable is a strong candidate for set/part/minifigure identity and bulk catalogue ingestion because it exposes official set catalogue data and bulk downloads. Its licence/terms must be reviewed before production ingestion and commercial use.

The product should maintain an `external_id_map` so catalogue provider identity can change without corrupting user inventory.

### 11.3 BrickLink

BrickLink is a candidate source for price-guide statistics and later user-store integration. Its commercial API constraints are material. Before any paid FlipSets feature uses BrickLink-integrated functionality, commercial use must be reviewed and, where prudent, written clarification requested.

**Never scrape BrickLink around the API.**

### 11.4 Retailers

Prefer, in order:

1. official affiliate/product feeds where terms fit;
2. documented retailer APIs;
3. licensed aggregators;
4. user-entered deal prices;
5. public-page retrieval only where technically and contractually acceptable.

Retail scraping is not a default architecture.

### 11.5 User truth

The user's own purchase receipts and realized sales can ultimately become the most valuable data for personalization. Those records must remain exportable and clearly distinguished from public market evidence.

## 13. USER EXPERIENCE

### 13.1 The 10-second rule

A returning user who knows the set number and price should reach a Deal Card in roughly one interaction sequence:

`SET NUMBER -> PRICE -> RESULT`

Saved defaults supply market, condition and cost profile.

### 13.2 Input modes — ordered by simplicity

1. type set number;
2. barcode scan;
3. search by name;
4. receipt import;
5. box-photo recognition;
6. shelf/multi-set recognition.

Do not start with the technically glamorous mode.

### 13.3 The visual hierarchy

On a Deal Card the eye should see, in order:

1. estimated net profit range;
2. buy price;
3. break-even sale price;
4. confidence/evidence quality;
5. holding/liquidity evidence when available;
6. detailed comps.

The user can drill down. The main screen remains fast.

### 13.4 Vocabulary

Potential in-product language:

- **Deal Check** — one evaluation;
- **True Margin** — margin after configured selling costs;
- **Break-even** — minimum sale price under assumptions;
- **Flip Range** — conservative/base/optimistic net outcome;
- **Evidence Grade** — quality of underlying market evidence;
- **My Flips** — acquired inventory intended for resale;
- **Capital Locked** — current cost basis in unsold inventory;
- **Sell-Through** — observed/estimated velocity;
- **Exit** — sale outcome, not a speculative promise.

Avoid making “FlipScore” the primary truth unless every component is inspectable. A pretty 87/100 score is less useful than €14–€22 net with Grade A evidence.

## 14. LEARNING LOOP

The most defensible version of FlipSets learns from the difference between **expected** and **realized** outcomes.

On acquisition, freeze the Deal Check assumptions.

On sale, record:

- actual sale price;
- actual fees;
- actual shipping;
- days held;
- actual return/refund impact;
- actual net profit.

Then compare:

`PREDICTED -> REALIZED -> ERROR -> PROFILE UPDATE`

Questions the system can eventually answer:

- Do this user's eBay sales realize 93% of median sold price?
- Does this user consistently underestimate shipping on large sets?
- Which themes move fastest for them?
- What buy-price bands produce their best capital turnover?
- Which marketplaces produce the best net result after all friction?

This is more useful than generic “AI investment advice.”

- deal calculation library;
- evidence-quality rules;
- static Deal Card mock;
- provider/legal research ledger.

**Acceptance:** a spreadsheet/test harness can reproduce Deal Card outputs deterministically.

**No login. No AI. No payments.**

**Purpose:** prove the core loop.

- manual buy price;
- saved market/fee defaults;
- one or more legal data sources;

- evidence drilldown;
- save/delete Deal Checks locally or under simple account auth.

**Success metric:** median time from opening app to useful result.

## 16. PARKING LOT — GOOD IDEAS THAT ARE NOT MVP

These are intentionally captured so they do not hitchhike into V1.

### Shelf Vision

Point the camera at a retail shelf, recognize many boxes, overlay deal economics.

### Lot Vision

Photograph a Facebook/2dehands-style mixed LEGO lot and estimate identifiable set/minifigure value.

### Part-Out Mode

Compare sealed/whole-set resale economics with estimated part-out economics. Requires careful labour assumptions; a theoretical part-out value is not profit.

### Minifigure Mode

Identify minifigures inside used lots and surface disproportionate value.

### Receipt Harvester

Extract set numbers, unit prices and discounts from receipts and convert selected lines into inventory lots.

### Capital Allocator

Given a budget, show how different candidate buys affect expected profit, liquidity and storage.

### Space Ledger

Box dimensions / storage bins / profit per storage volume per month.

### Liquidation Mode

Identify stale inventory and calculate realistic exit options.

### Browser Companion

Evaluate products while browsing supported retailers. Valuable, but BrickPicker already proves the category and browser support adds maintenance burden.

### Deal Feed

A personalized stream of opportunities. This comes only after source rights and evidence quality are solved.

### Community Deal Sharing

Potentially useful, but susceptible to spam, affiliate abuse, pumping and stale deals. Parked.

## 17. OTHRYS / VTC RELATIONSHIP

FlipSets is a **separate product/Oros**.

It should not live as a LEGO mode inside VTC.

VTC remains the vintage-toy collector product. FlipSets is a transactional reseller decision product. Their domains overlap at collector primitives but their user jobs and data models differ.

Reuse policy:

`SEARCH ESTATE -> IDENTIFY GENERIC CAPABILITY -> EXTRACT/USE BLOCK -> ADAPT PRODUCT SHELL -> BUILD ONLY TRUE GAP`

Candidate reusable capabilities from VTC/OTHRYS include:

- account/auth patterns;
- user-photo pipeline;
- AI-provider routing;
- scan cost controls;
- event/analytics logging;
- notification patterns;
- provenance conventions;
- wallet/payment gating patterns only after Money Gate unlock;
- marketplace integration patterns where generic.

Do **not** copy VTC's figure-specific schema, badges, forum, wiki, marketplace or visual identity by default.

FlipSets should also feed improvements back into generic OTHRYS Blocks when they truly generalize, especially:

- fee-profile calculation;
- evidence/provenance grading;
- product-ID reconciliation;
- alerting;
- receipt parsing;
- realized-vs-predicted evaluation.

## 18. BRAND AND IP GATE

### Working name

`FlipSets` is a strong concept placeholder. It has not been declared legally clear or finally protectable.

Before public launch:

- exact and fuzzy EUIPO search;
- BOIP search;
- relevant national/international marks;
- company/trade-name search;
- domains;
- App Store / Play Store;
- social handles;
- legal review if ambiguity remains.

### LEGO trademark

Do not put `LEGO` in the product/domain name.

LEGO's published Fair Play guidance states that the LEGO logo should not be used on an unofficial site, discourages incorporation of the LEGO trademark into an internet address, and asks for a disclaimer when the trademark appears. It also notes that LEGO catalog/package photographs and instructions are copyrighted.

FlipSets should therefore:

- use its own identity;
- never use the LEGO logo as product branding;
- display a clear non-affiliation statement;
- license or legitimately source images/data;
- avoid implying endorsement;
- re-check commercial-use implications before launch because LEGO's enthusiast guidance is framed primarily around non-commercial use.

Working disclaimer concept:

> LEGO® is a trademark of the LEGO Group of companies, which does not sponsor, authorize or endorse FlipSets.

Final legal copy requires review before commercial launch.

## 19. THE MONEY GATE

### STATE: **CLOSED**

The Money Gate is a product and technical boundary, not a reminder note.

While closed, FlipSets may be researched, designed, coded, locally tested, privately demonstrated and used with mock/sandbox economics. It may not intentionally generate business revenue.

### 19.1 Forbidden while CLOSED

- Stripe or other live subscription charging;
- paid app access;
- paid feature unlocks;
- affiliate commissions;
- referral commissions;
- advertising revenue;
- sponsored placements;
- accepting tips/donations through FlipSets;
- taking commission on third-party sales;
- brokering payments;
- selling market data;
- paid reseller services;
- commercial customer contracts;
- silently turning on an existing VTC payment rail;
- production secrets for payment providers;
- a “temporary workaround” through another person's account/business.

### 19.2 Allowed while CLOSED

- pricing-page mockups clearly marked non-live;
- fake/test subscription plans;
- Stripe test mode if needed for learning, with no live charges;
- simulated affiliate attribution with zero real commission;
- unit-economics modelling;
- willingness-to-pay interviews;
- waitlists;
- free private alpha/beta;
- technical gates and feature flags;
- research into legal/company/VAT requirements;
- non-monetized public demo only after brand/data/privacy requirements are separately satisfied.

### 19.3 Technical enforcement

At minimum:

```text
MONEY_ENABLED=false
AFFILIATE_ENABLED=false
ADS_ENABLED=false
LIVE_PAYMENTS_ENABLED=false
MARKETPLACE_COMMISSION_ENABLED=false
```

Server-side authority must enforce the gate. A client-side hidden button is not a gate.

Any revenue-generating endpoint must fail closed unless the canonical commercial state explicitly authorizes it.

### 19.4 Unlock prerequisites

Money Gate may move from `CLOSED` to `READY_FOR_OPERATOR_DECISION` only when all applicable prerequisites have evidence:

1. **Operator authorization** — explicit decision to commercialize FlipSets.
2. **Personal/status compatibility** — training, benefit, employment or other status checked where relevant; no assumption that software income is harmless.
3. **Business setup** — appropriate Belgian business form/registration confirmed.
4. **VAT** — VAT treatment confirmed and registration/activation handled where required.
5. **Consumer law** — subscription/digital-service terms, cancellation, conformity and refund obligations reviewed.
6. **Privacy/GDPR** — data inventory, lawful basis, retention, deletion/export and processors documented.
7. **Terms of Service** — usable terms published.
8. **Provider rights** — every material data/API/image source cleared for the intended commercial use.
9. **Trademark/brand** — final name cleared to an acceptable level.
10. **Payments** — live payment processor account owned by the proper business entity; tax/invoice data configured.
11. **Accounting** — revenue/fees/VAT/refunds can be reconciled.
12. **Security** — secrets, auth, webhook validation, access controls and logs verified.
13. **Product honesty** — no guaranteed-profit claims; methodology and evidence limitations visible.
14. **Support path** — contact and basic support process exists.
15. **Go-live rollback** — one switch can disable commercial activity without taking the entire informational product offline.

Only after these are satisfied may the operator explicitly set the commercial gate to `OPEN`.

### 19.5 Belgian baseline captured during research

Belgian government guidance states that a sole proprietorship is registered through the Crossroads Bank for Enterprises (CBE/KBO), and that where an activity is subject to VAT the enterprise number must be activated for VAT **before** the VAT-taxable activity starts. The Belgian government also notes a small-enterprise VAT exemption possibility below the published annual-revenue threshold, subject to its rules. Digital paid services also fall under EU/Belgian consumer conformity protections.

These facts are gate inputs, not a determination of the operator's future tax/status treatment.

## 20. MONETIZATION — PARKED BEHIND THE GATE

The likely primary model after legal/commercial unlock is **subscription**, because FlipSets can directly save or make the user money.

Do not set final prices before usage data.

### Free hypothesis

- set lookup;
- basic Deal Checks;
- manual fee profile;
- limited saved deals;
- clear evidence labels;
- manual realized-sale entry.

### Pro hypothesis

Potential willingness-to-pay range to test: approximately **€5–€12/month**, not canon.

Candidate paid value:

- live/expanded evidence feeds;
- real-time deal alerts;
- unlimited saved checks;
- multiple fee/selling profiles;
- inventory aging;
- realized-vs-predicted analytics;
- receipt OCR;
- bulk tools;
- richer market comparisons;
- export.

BrickPicker's current $29.99/month Premium price proves that serious users may pay materially more for a complete reseller suite, but FlipSets should not price itself as a complete suite before becoming one.

### Later Business hypothesis

Only if demanded:

- multi-location;
- bulk receiving;
- seller integrations;
- advanced accounting export;
- team seats;
- API;
- high-volume automation.

### Affiliate revenue

Potentially useful for retailer links, but secondary and transparent. Never allow affiliate economics to contaminate the deal recommendation.

A worse deal must never rank above a better deal because it pays FlipSets commission.

### Ads

Not part of the initial commercial thesis. Ads can corrupt trust in a decision product and are unlikely to be worth the distraction early.

## 21. BUSINESS METRICS

Do not optimize vanity traffic before decision quality.

- Deal Checks per active user;

- predicted vs realized profit error;

- free -> paid conversion;
- monthly recurring revenue;
- churn;
- gross margin after data/AI costs;
- acquisition cost;
- revenue per active reseller;

## 22. AI POLICY

FlipSets should be mostly deterministic.

Use AI for:

- visual set recognition fallback;
- receipt extraction;
- fuzzy product matching;
- messy marketplace-title normalization;
- explanation of evidence in plain language;
- anomaly detection;
- later, optional natural-language search.

Do not use AI to invent market prices or unsupported retirement dates.

Preferred recognition cascade:

`BARCODE/SET NUMBER -> CATALOG LOOKUP -> FUZZY MATCH -> VISION -> HUMAN CONFIRMATION`

This mirrors the useful lesson already learned in VTC: paid vision should not be the first tool when deterministic identity is available.

## 23. TRUST AND SAFETY

### No guaranteed-return language

Forbidden product copy:

- “Guaranteed profit”
- “Risk-free LEGO investment”
- “This set will rise 40%”

Preferred:

- “Recent sold evidence suggests…”
- “Estimated net outcome under your saved fee profile…”
- “Evidence is sparse.”
- “Future resale value is uncertain.”

### Source transparency

Whenever possible, expose:

- evidence source;
- recency;
- market;
- sample size;
- condition;
- assumptions.

### Conflicts

If affiliate links or sponsorship are later enabled, recommendations must remain ranking-independent from compensation unless explicitly labelled and intentionally separated.

### User control

Users can override assumptions. FlipSets explains the consequence.

## 24. SECURITY AND PRIVACY BASELINE

FlipSets is initially low-risk, but seller integrations and receipts can become sensitive.

Rules:

- never store marketplace passwords;
- OAuth tokens encrypted/server-side where integrations require them;
- least-privilege scopes;
- receipts private by default;
- payment data delegated to payment processor, not stored directly;
- data export and deletion paths;
- no public inventory/location by default;
- audit important account/integration changes;
- no production secrets in repo;
- distinguish anonymous market evidence from identifiable user transaction history.

Physical storage addresses/locations must never become accidentally public through shareable portfolio pages.

## 27. ACCEPTANCE TEST FOR THE PRODUCT THESIS

FlipSets earns Phase 2 only if a small beta can demonstrate all of the following:

- users can perform a Deal Check quickly enough to use while shopping;
- users understand the output without training;
- users trust the evidence because they can inspect it;
- at least some checks materially change buy/skip decisions;
- realized sale records show the calculation is directionally useful;
- users voluntarily return for subsequent buying decisions;
- the data cost per check is sustainable;
- no critical provider-rights blocker makes the core evidence unavailable.

If these do not hold, do not hide the failure by adding features.

## 32. THE ONE-PAGE VERSION

**Problem**

People see LEGO sets on sale and confuse discount/spread with profit. Fees, shipping, weak comps, slow demand and locked capital make many “good deals” mediocre.

**Product**

FlipSets evaluates the deal in front of the user.

**Input**

Set + buy price + condition + selling profile.

**Output**

Realistic resale range + selling costs + break-even + estimated net profit/ROI + evidence grade.

**Differentiation**

Fast mobile decision, transparent evidence, local-market correctness, and later learning from the user's own realized flips.

**MVP**

Responsive PWA with a Deal Card.

**Expansion**

My Flips -> realized P&L -> scan/OCR -> Benelux deal feeds -> alerts -> selling copilot -> advanced reseller OS.

**Business model**

Subscription is the primary hypothesis, affiliate revenue secondary, ads not initial strategy.

**Commercial state**

**MONEY GATE CLOSED.** Build and learn first. Commercialization is a separate explicit decision with legal, provider, business, consumer, privacy, accounting and security prerequisites.

**North star**

> **FLIPSETS — Know the flip before you buy.**
